Archive Suite · what the bench has measured

Archive OCR bench

Six parts: the W41 OCR panel ( routes reading the same 96 truth pages), segmentation (how well each approach splits a box of photographs into documents), the on-device handwriting readers the Vision OCR lab measured, open-weight models run on Stanford's FarmShare GPUs, the panel's pages ranked by difficulty, with cheap-first cascades simulated from the measured readings, and how well the tagger dates a document. The page is rebuilt from the bench's result files whenever a measured result lands.

Part 1 · OCR panel · 96 truth pages

OCR price and accuracy

Each point is one route: its NES score against what 1,000 pages cost. NES is character edit similarity to the adjudicated truth, so 96 means about 4% of characters would need fixing. On the charts a failed page scores 0; the ranking table first gives the score on answered pages, the headline, with the failures beside it. Where a provider sells batch processing, the route appears twice, at its standard and batch price, joined by a dotted line. Some routes ran on our own hardware rather than a paid endpoint. Those on the owner's Mac sit in the free column. Every open-weight model read on these 96 pages on Stanford's FarmShare cluster is here too. FarmShare bills nothing, so a FarmShare run of a model OpenRouter sells (□) is priced at what OpenRouter charges for the same model, from the run's own measured tokens. A run of a model OpenRouter does not sell (✶: GLM-OCR, LightOnOCR-3, PaddleOCR-VL 1.5, PP-OCRv6, CHURRO-3B, Qwen3.5 4B) has no hosted price: it sits in the free column, just right of the Mac routes, as free on a university cluster, and its GPU-hours are in the tables. Native rows use their model card's own prompt, as in part 4. A route on our own hardware that is the same model as a hosted route is listed in the tables under that route.

The ranking: accuracy on the pages each route answered

The headline score is NES on the pages a route answered, with its failures beside it: a page that fails goes on to another reader, so it is not inaccurate text. The charts below score a failed page 0, which let a route's failures, not its reading, set the top of the ranking (the measurement audit's OCR-2, 2026-10-30). Failures are split: provider is the provider or the connection losing the request (a 5xx, an interrupted or broken stream), so the model never answered; model is the model's own (caps: ran to the token cap, nearly always a loop; refusals: a content filter, or Gemini's refusal of reprinted text; empty: stopped with nothing usable). The paired column is the route less Qwen3.6 27B (the local panel's hosted reading) on the pages both answered, with a 95% bootstrap interval over pages; an interval that crosses 0 is a tie, and so is a gap under twice the rerun spread (the next panel). Page p081 is reported apart, since the owner cannot correct its truth. A route marked "96 only" has not read the 895 public pages itself, so it is missing from the comparison on independent truth (W43.sweep holds most hosted routes). Source: ocr-pages.csv.

What flags an answered page that is wrong

A cascade sees a failure but not a poor reading. AUC is how well a signal ranks a reader's poor readings above its good ones: 0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect. The year and month the tagger gives a document

Against the owner's tags

What is scoredDocumentsRightShare

Segmentation only proposes boundaries; the tagging call dates each document afterwards, with the prompt TagGenerator.generateTags sends (year always, month only if the document states it, date_uncertain when the year is not in the document itself). Documents are runs of pages with one identity tag set, kept only where the label-clean teacher and the tags agree on every gap around them, sampled across collections; the corpus copies of the test and development boxes are left out. The text sent is ABBYY's layer, not the app's own OCR. A month the reply gives where the owner tagged none is not always invented: most of those months are named in the text, so the owner left them untagged. Open line recognisers for the hardest strata (W43.script-specialists), read on our Mac with kraken: its default blla segmenter cuts each page into lines once, and every model reads the same lines, so a model's score includes the segmenter's reading order. That costs a line model against a whole-page reader, which chrF, nearly blind to order, shows. "Unknown" means the model's record gives no training list to check against our sources; a named source means the model was trained on that test source, so its score is an upper bound, not a specialist's. BiblIA's model writes lines in visual order and is reversed to logical order before scoring. Same pages and scorer as the table above; no money. FarmShare now supplies CHURRO-3B (its own XML prompt), Qari and Fanar on Arabic pages, and the Bern Qwen model on the same German line crops. Capped or malformed replies count as failures; any missing or capped German line fails its page. CHURRO has known source overlap on L5, L7 and L8. The German adapter has an unstated licence and possible Hufeland overlap, so it is scored only; the survey is ArchiveProcessor/segbench-results/bench-corpus/specialists.md.

OCR costs. Measured charges for the panel's pinned OpenRouter endpoint (Mistral's published rate for Mistral OCR), as dollars per 1,000 pages, averaged over all 96 pages; the same route cost is used on every chart, so the kind charts compare accuracy at a route's average price. Apple Vision runs on the device and costs nothing, and so do the three models read on our Mac (an M3 Pro with 18 GB): Qwen3-VL 8B (about 41 seconds a page), LightOnOCR-3-4B (about 24) and Qwen3.5-9B (about 38). Qwen3.6 27B ran on FarmShare, which bills nothing, but it does not fit on the Mac, so it is priced at OpenRouter's list rate for the same model ($0.25 and $2 per million input and output tokens, read 2026-10-20) applied to its own measured tokens; its GPU time is in the tables as GPU-hours per 100,000 photos. Claude Sonnet 5.5 ran through the subscription; its price is an estimate from its token counts at $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens, using the standard image size, and was not measured.

Batch prices. Half the measured cost, where OpenRouter lists a batch version of the model at half its standard price (Gemini 3.8 Flash's batch price is half of what the run paid). Mistral OCR's batch price follows Mistral's published 50% batch discount. Routes with no batch point have no batch version in the catalogue.

Limits. One corpus of about 100 pages. Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash drafted the truth before it was adjudicated, so it may lean toward those families. The "local panel" was chosen for models that could run on a Mac, and ran hosted; two of them have been measured on the Mac itself: Qwen3-VL 8B lost 2.15 points, nearly all of them on four pages it failed to finish, and Qwen3.5-9B gained 2.48, because the hosted route failed four pages and the Mac one; on handwritten pages the Mac's Qwen3.5-9B is 3.0 points below its hosted run. LightOnOCR-3-4B has no hosted route to compare. The FarmShare route is the same model and precision as the hosted Qwen3.6 27B and differs by about a point, which is within noise. Differences of a point or two are not a ranking, and the handwritten chart rests on about 2,200 words.
