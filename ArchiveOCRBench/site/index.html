Part 2 · Segmentation · two locked test boxes, 363 pages

Segmentation: splitting a box into documents

A box is photographed page by page, and the app has to say where one document ends and the next begins. Each approach is scored on two test boxes (Stanton and RG 165) held out until its settings were frozen on three development boxes. "No checking" is the share of document pages that land in exactly the right document if nobody checks anything. Each approach also flags the boundaries it is unsure of. Checking those flags costs the owner's time, so the table counts them per 100 pages and says how many pages end up right after checking.

Part 3 · Handwriting on the Mac · Vision OCR lab

A different truth set and a different score from part 1, so these numbers do not compare with NES. Missed words are truth words the reader did not produce; added words are words it produced that match no truth word. The pick rule was set before the run: of the readers that pass, take the fewest missed, but where two are within 20 per 1,000, take the smaller and faster. Qwen3.5-9B and LightOnOCR-3-4B are 5 apart, so LightOnOCR-3-4B, which is nearly twice as fast, is the pick (owner confirmed 2026-10-15, Vision OCR commit 168af14). Churro's added words come from one page that looped. As read, neither model returns word positions, and the app's alignment places only 65% of their words, so handwritten pages will get a separate text page.

OCR costs. Measured charges for the panel's pinned OpenRouter endpoint (Mistral's published rate for Mistral OCR), as dollars per 1,000 pages, averaged over all 96 pages; the same route cost is used on every chart, so the kind charts compare accuracy at a route's average price. Apple Vision runs on the device and costs nothing. Claude Sonnet 5.5 ran through the subscription; its price is an estimate from its token counts at $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens, using the standard image size, and was not measured.

Batch prices. Half the measured cost, where OpenRouter lists a batch version of the model at half its standard price (Gemini 3.8 Flash's batch price is half of what the run paid). Mistral OCR's batch price follows Mistral's published 50% batch discount. Routes with no batch point have no batch version in the catalogue.

Limits. One corpus of about 100 pages. Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash drafted the truth before it was adjudicated, so it may lean toward those families. The "local panel" was chosen for models that could run on a Mac, but every route here ran hosted; Mac quality is unmeasured. Differences of a point or two are not a ranking, and the handwritten chart rests on about 2,200 words.
