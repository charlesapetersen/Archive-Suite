Part 2 · Segmentation · two locked test boxes, 363 pages

Segmentation: splitting a box into documents

A box is photographed page by page, and the app has to say where one document ends and the next begins. Each approach is scored on two test boxes (Stanton and RG 165) held out until its settings were frozen on three development boxes. "No checking" is the share of document pages that land in exactly the right document if nobody checks anything. Each approach also flags the boundaries it is unsure of. Checking those flags costs the owner's time, so the table counts them per 100 pages and says how many pages end up right after checking.

Handwriting readers that run on the device

Missed and added words

A different truth set and a different score from part 1, so these numbers do not compare with NES. Missed words are truth words the reader did not produce; added words are words it produced that match no truth word. The pick rule was set before the run: of the readers that pass, take the fewest missed, but where two are within 20 per 1,000, take the smaller and faster. Qwen3.5-9B and LightOnOCR-3-4B are 5 apart, so LightOnOCR-3-4B, which is nearly twice as fast, is the pick (owner confirmed 2026-10-15, Vision OCR commit 168af14). Churro's added words come from one page that looped. Both are also on part 1's charts as Mac routes (◇), read on the panel's truth set and scored by NES like every route: on its handwritten chart LightOnOCR-3-4B scores 89.67 and Qwen3.5-9B 87.92 at 4-bit (90.92 hosted). As read, neither model returns word positions, and the app's alignment places only 65% of their words, so handwritten pages will get a separate text page.

Part 5 · Page difficulty and cascades · the same 96 pages

Accuracy on the pages answered, and what to do with the rest

A page that fails is not inaccurate text: it goes on to another model. So here accuracy on the pages a route answered and its failures are counted apart, with page difficulty in the picture, and cascades send each page to a cheap reader first and on to a stronger one when it fails or looks wrong. Every figure is from the panel's measured readings; no new calls were made. The full report, with every table, is ArchiveOCRBench/difficulty.md.

What flags an answered page that is wrong

A cascade sees a failure but not a poor reading. AUC is how well a signal ranks a reader's poor readings above its good ones: 0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect. Length compares the reader's word count with Apple Vision's (free, on the device); disagreement compares its text with a second cheap reader's; kind is the reader's own handwritten-or-mixed label; Vision is whether Vision OCR's page sorter calls the page handwriting, the same for every reader. No route reports a confidence.

Vision OCR's page kind, a label no model gives

free, on the Mac, before any reading

The Vision OCR lab's page sorter calls a page newspaper, photograph, handwriting or other from Apple Vision's reading of it: its lines' number and width, the paper's light and colour, and the shape of the ink. It needs no model and no paid call, and it does not depend on the route being judged. On the panel's accuracy pages, against the truth's kind, with the mean NES of all routes:

OCR costs. Measured charges for the panel's pinned OpenRouter endpoint (Mistral's published rate for Mistral OCR), as dollars per 1,000 pages, averaged over all 96 pages; the same route cost is used on every chart, so the kind charts compare accuracy at a route's average price. Apple Vision runs on the device and costs nothing, and so do the three models read on our Mac (an M3 Pro with 18 GB): Qwen3-VL 8B (about 41 seconds a page), LightOnOCR-3-4B (about 24) and Qwen3.5-9B (about 38). Qwen3.6 27B on FarmShare is priced at $0 because the cluster bills nothing; what it costs is GPU time, shown in the tables as GPU-hours per 100,000 photos. Claude Sonnet 5.5 ran through the subscription; its price is an estimate from its token counts at $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens, using the standard image size, and was not measured.

Batch prices. Half the measured cost, where OpenRouter lists a batch version of the model at half its standard price (Gemini 3.8 Flash's batch price is half of what the run paid). Mistral OCR's batch price follows Mistral's published 50% batch discount. Routes with no batch point have no batch version in the catalogue.

Limits. One corpus of about 100 pages. Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash drafted the truth before it was adjudicated, so it may lean toward those families. The "local panel" was chosen for models that could run on a Mac, and ran hosted; two of them have been measured on the Mac itself: Qwen3-VL 8B lost 2.15 points, nearly all of them on four pages it failed to finish, and Qwen3.5-9B gained 2.48, because the hosted route failed four pages and the Mac one; on handwritten pages the Mac's Qwen3.5-9B is 3.0 points below its hosted run. LightOnOCR-3-4B has no hosted route to compare. The FarmShare route is the same model and precision as the hosted Qwen3.6 27B and differs by about a point, which is within noise. Differences of a point or two are not a ranking, and the handwritten chart rests on about 2,200 words.
