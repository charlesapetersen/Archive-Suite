# W49.proc-fix-s042 — a failed tagging call is reported, not filed as tagged (S-042)

2026-11-20 · `scripts/test-tagging-failure.sh` and the W40.d1 stand-in journey in the VM · $0, key-free.

## What was wrong

`TagGenerator.generateTags` turned a failed call (a throw, or a reply it could not parse) into the fallback
`GeneratedTags(dateUncertain: true)` and said nothing. The document was filed with no subject tags, its job stayed
`succeeded`, and the status line counted it: W40.d1's cap test (2026-11-18) filed `00012 — RG 165.pdf` untagged
under "11 segments tagged".

## The fix

- `TagGenerator.generateTagsReporting` returns the tags and why the call gave none: the error (after
  `NetworkSession`'s own retries), or "the reply was not the tag JSON" (no JSON object, or one with neither
  `subject_tags` nor `year`, such as an `{"error": …}` body with a 200). `generateTags` is unchanged for its other
  callers (Live Capture, the institutional-compute import).
- **The retry route is the existing one, unchanged.** `NetworkSession` retries a 429 for every call, and a 5xx or a
  timeout only for idempotent ones; a paid tagging call is not idempotent, so neither is retried there (the provider
  may already have charged it), and the tagging phase adds no retry of its own. The final review's **Redo** is the
  route that asks again; its pass sets the record afresh, so it clears what its calls answer.
- **What is filed is unchanged:** the output is still written (its OCR text is the work) with the fallback tags and
  whatever the dating route found.
- `performTaggingPhase` records each failure in `OCRProcessor.taggingCallFailures`, keyed by the document's first
  input URL (two inputs with one name in different folders are two). The status line says "k of N segments tagged. ⚠️ k
  documents' tagging calls failed (names): reason — they were filed with no subject tags from the model. Re-run them
  to tag them."; the run log lists every one with its reason; the final review's line says Redo asks again; an output
  removed in review takes its record with it. A gateway refusal's reason now names its HTTP status.
