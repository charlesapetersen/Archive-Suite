`CaptureViewModel.addDocumentPhoto`, and let the real `DriveClient` and `DriveRelayTransport`

A separate Python peer sent two real fixture JPEGs to the built Mac Processor via the local relay.
The app ran headlessly with on-device Vision, merge on, and all backup, relay and output paths in
a temporary directory. The first case sent both photos and the segment completion before Finish.
The second held the second photo and its completion on the phone side, as the real clients defer
completion until the photos are receipted. The headless session marker called the same
`requestFinish` and `proceedToFinishIfReady` that the Finish button uses.

| Relay delivery | Image sheets filed | PDF pages, including text | Second photo |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Both photos before Finish | 2 | 4 | In the filed PDF |
| Second photo held until Finish filed | 1 | 2 | Receipted after Finish and kept in Backup |

The retained late-arrival run filed in 2.70 seconds. `manifest.json` then listed sequence 2 under the filed
group and the backed-up JPEG existed. The sole filed PDF remained byte-identical. Both local and
Drive relay use `FileRelayReceiver`. Neither relay supplies the pending count to
`CaptureSession.updatePhonePending`, which the LAN heartbeat feeds. The 20-second expiry for an
old heartbeat does not account for this short run, because the relay sent no heartbeat at all. The offline recovery suite passed 300 checks,
including the control in which a fresh pending count holds Finish after the tag card is resolved.
