# W40.fix-reader-fts-snippet-stall — a multi-word search with common words at corpus size

2026-10-31 · checkpoint `694e025f`, then the completing commit · builder's run, not the recheck (builder is not
verifier).

**Cause.** W40.d7 measured `searchRanked("Bureau of the Budget")` at 41.7 s over 102,478 rows, of which the MATCH
was 24 ms and the rest `snippet()` over the top hits, about 200 ms a hit. FTS5's `snippet()` scores candidate
fragments against every instance of every phrase in the document, so its cost grows with the square of the
instance count. `of` and `the` occur thousands of times in a long OCR body.

**What changed** (`Search/ContentIndex.swift`, `Search/SearchSnippet.swift`).
- `snippet()` is given `snippetMatchExpression`: the query without `SearchSnippet.stopWords` (or all of it when
  every term is one). The MATCH that decides the hits and their bm25 order is unchanged. Dropping AND-ed terms
  only widens a match, so every hit still matches the snippet expression; the stop words are just not highlighted.
- Each hit's snippet is one prepared statement, `MATCH ? AND rowid = ?`, best match first, instead of one
  `path IN (…)` query.
- Snippet work stops after `SearchSnippet.timeBudget` (250 ms). Rows left get no preview, as rows past the
  300-snippet limit already did. Every path is still returned. This bounds what a query whose distinctive words
  are themselves frequent can hold the `ContentIndex` actor for.

**Run.** `ReaderAtSizeTests.testCommonWordPhraseSnippetsStayFastOnLongBodies`, which needs no harness: 60 bodies of
about 14,000 words, `of`/`the` about 6,000 times each, the phrase five times. Debug build, app-hosted.

| `searchRanked("Bureau of the Budget")`, 60 long bodies | previews | time |
|---|---|---|
| old snippet expression (the fix reverted for one run) | 2 of 60 (budget ran out, ~135 ms a document) | 273 ms, **TEST FAILED** |
| this change | 60 of 60 | **25.8 ms** |

The test asserts every hit keeps its preview and the call stays under 500 ms, so it fails before the fix (rule 8).
`testIndexSearchAndHealthOnTheRealSample` also asserts `Bureau of the Budget` under 1 s at 102k rows.

**Verdict: the fix holds at corpus size.** `Bureau of the Budget` went from 41.7 s to 0.27 s at 102k rows, every
one of its 215 hits with a preview; `testIndexSearchAndHealthOnTheRealSample` asserts it under 1 s and passed.
That is about 20 ms of match and 250 ms of snippets, so this query uses up the snippet budget [I: total minus match]
and a slightly longer one would lose the last previews, not stall. Single distinctive words are within a few ms to
tens of ms of before; that difference is one prepared statement a hit in place of one `IN` query, and is not a cost
anyone sees.

**Previews, read with care.** `the` gets 8 previews at 102k and 132 on the sample: it is all stop words, so its
snippet expression is the whole query and the 250 ms budget runs out; that is the bound working. `Kennedy` (0 of
300 at 102k, in 41 ms) and `president` (20 of 300, ~140 ms of snippets) finish inside the budget, so the missing
previews are rows whose snippet is empty and is dropped: with name ×5 and classification ×10 in bm25, rows that
match in the file name and have no OCR body rank first, and replicated 19 times they fill the top 300 [I: from the
weights and the code, which drops empty snippets before and after the fix; the rows were not listed]. The before
run did not count previews, so it is not shown that the old code gave more for these queries; by the code it gave
the same. Not a regression from this fix; whether the top 300 should prefer rows with a body is a ranking question
for the bench, not filed.
