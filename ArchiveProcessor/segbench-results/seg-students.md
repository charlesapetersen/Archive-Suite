# W41.seg-finetune — the fine-tuned Mac segmenters on the locked test boxes

Generated by `ArchiveProcessor/scripts/segbench/method_openrouter.py report-students` from FarmShare runs (one L40S,
vLLM 0.31.0, bf16; `farmshare/fs_seg.py pack --final`, imported with `fs_seg.py import`). Qwen3.5 4B and 2B, LoRA
fine-tuned (`farmshare/train_seg.py`, rank 16, one epoch) on 5,885 corpus windows whose every gap the teacher
(Qwen3.5 27B, `seg-teacher.md`) and the owner's tags agree on, with the teacher's own reply as the target and 29%
of windows sent without page text (`corpus-pairs.md`). Frozen in `seg-students.json` on the development boxes and
the corpus's held-out collections before the one test-box call. Scored as `cheap-models.md` and `seg-teacher.md`
score, beside Gemini 3.8 Flash's cached windows. No money.

## On the Mac (W41.seg-mac-model)

Each model merged on FarmShare (`train_seg.py merge`), converted here with the Vision OCR lab's `convert-mlx.py`
(mlx-vlm 0.7.4) at 8 and 4 bits, and run on the 18 GB M3 Pro through `method_local.py collect` under the lab's
memory guard (`run-guarded.sh`), the bench's own Mac route (1024 px pages, temperature 0, thinking off). The
development boxes only: the test boxes were scored once, at full precision, above. Pages in the exact document,
no checking; seconds a seven-page window; peak memory as MLX reports it; size on disk.

| build | Dean | Deaver | Herrnstein | pooled (551) | false splits | false merges | s a window | peak GB | size |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2B, 4-bit (6.2 bits a weight) | 88.8% | 75.2% | 90.1% | 85.6% | 2 | 25 | 15 | 3.6 | 1.6 GB |
| **2B, 8-bit** (9.6) | 92.3% | 90.1% | 88.4% | **90.3%** | 6 | 11 | 18 | 4.4 | 2.5 GB |
| 4B, 4-bit (5.3) | 81.6% | 87.9% | 90.6% | 86.6% | 14 | 9 | 31 | 5.5 | 2.8 GB |
| 4B, 8-bit (9.1) | 84.2% | 88.7% | 91.2% | 88.0% | 7 | 10 | 37 | 7.3 | 4.8 GB |

For comparison on the same three boxes: the 2B at full precision on FarmShare about 90.5%, the 27B teacher 90.2%,
Gemini 3.8 Flash 90.7%, and the untrained 4B at 4-bit on this Mac 57.4% on Deaver (`local-models-Deaver.md`).

- **The 8-bit 2B is the one to ship.** It keeps its full-precision accuracy (90.3% against 90.5%), is the most
  accurate of the four builds, needs 4.4 GB at its peak and 2.5 GB of download, and reads about 6 seconds a page
  (each page sits in about 2.3 windows): roughly 1.7 hours for 1,000 pages on the M3 Pro.
- **4-bit costs two to five points** (mostly false merges for the 2B: 25 against 11) for a saving of under a
  gigabyte, so it is not worth offering.
- **The 4B buys nothing here.** Twice the time and 3 GB more memory for a lower pooled score; at full precision on
  the held-out collections it led the 2B by 1.6 points (97.6% against 96.0%) and on the test boxes by 0.3.
- An 8 GB Mac running the app beside a 4.4 GB peak is unmeasured; a 16 GB Mac has room.

## Reading

- **Training worked, and most of it came from the first quarter of the data.** Untrained, the 4B split documents
  everywhere (1,258 false splits on the held-out gaps); fine-tuned, it agrees with the cleaned labels on 97.6% of
  held-out gaps, and the pilot on a quarter of the windows already reached 97.1%.
- **On the test boxes the students land below their teacher.** 79.1% (4B) and 78.8% (2B) of test pages in the exact
  document unchecked, against the teacher's 83.0% and Gemini's 88.8%. Stanton is the hard box for all three Qwen
  models; RG 165 is 113 pages and swings widely (the 2B 94.3%, the 4B 79.3%).
- **Their confidences are no flag.** As with the teacher, reaching 98% after checking takes about 80 checks per 100
  pages, close to checking every gap.
- **The training data is one owner's archives.** The held-out collections are other collections from the same
  owner's corpus, so their 97% says the model carries across his collections, not to other people's. The test
  boxes, which no training window saw, are the better guide, and there the students are about ten points behind the
  best cloud model.
