# Does a straightened reading copy, made after OCR, make the text layer easier to select? (W46.place-geom)

Generated by `scripts/segbench/place_geom.py` (tables between the markers; `place_geom.py report` rewrites only
those). OCR stays on the raw photograph (W46.enh-dewarp). Here the same Apple Vision words, read once on the raw
image, are carried onto each arm's image through that arm's saved map and written as the PDF text layer there; the
owner's placement truth lines are carried the same way and dragged in PDFKit, one drag per line (F-placement §6.2,
"Selection is a line"). No model read a page, nothing left the Mac and no money was spent.

## Verdict

- **Deskew only is the one arm that helps, and it helps where the page is skewed.** Turning the raw photograph level
  by its median line angle, on a canvas enlarged so nothing is cut, with today's lab writer: line pass **+2.2 points
  over every page** (73.6% → 75.8%, paired 95% interval +1.1 to +3.6), **+6.8 on the 17 pages skewed 1° or more**
  (+3.4 to +14.2), +5.4 on perspective and +5.1 on curled pages, **+0.6 on level pages** and exactly 0 on the 16
  PDF images, which are already level. It removes no word. The same holds when the drags overshoot the ink
  (+1.7 overall, +5.4 skewed).
- **The quad and UVDoc copies lose lines and remove content.** Quad −5.7 (−9.8 to −1.8), UVDoc −8.6 (−12.4 to
  −5.0) overall, and neither wins on any stratum, curled pages included. Quad cuts 14 own-sheet truth words and 24
  of neighbour text on 12 pages; UVDoc cuts 289 own-sheet words and 27 of neighbour text on 38 pages, which the
  reading-copy rule forbids. So no straighten-after-OCR option is offered with perspective correction or dewarp.
- **One sloped run per line on the raw image (OCRmyPDF's design) loses to today's level runs**, −7.4 (−10.7 to −4.2),
  and loses on every arm. PDFKit reads a run drawn at about 1° as one line per word in `selectionsByLine`, and a drag
  across a sloped run on the skewed pages fails more often than one across a level run; the lab writer's level run
  over the line's box is the better design for PDFKit. The `level` control (same writer as `slope`, drawn level)
  is worse still (−15.8), so the lab writer's advantage is its own fitting, not only its levelness.
- **Recommendation:** a reading copy turned level after OCR, triggered by the page's measured median line angle of
  1° or more (not by a capture tag), on an enlarged canvas, with the lab writer's layer. An app change, so it is
  for the processing review's Phase 4 (W46.review-phase4), which files the implementation queue. Find is unchanged by every arm (86.2% everywhere, ±0.8), as expected: Find matches words,
  not lines.

The scorer is the line-drag pass on `place_functional.swift` (`layer`, `drag`), which W46.place-print and
W46.place-hands-lines reuse. Its own oracle passes 98.5% of 1,772 level lines in place and 11.4% with the layer
moved down a line; a sloped oracle passes 89.9%, the PDFKit cost of a sloped run measured on its own.

## Measurement card

- **Truth.**
  - The placement truth (`place-truth.md`): 40 owner-typed and 18 hand pages of the 96. Typed lines are 51%
    Vision-derived (Vision's lines, boxes trimmed to the ink) and 49% drawn by Claude Opus 5.5 subagents
    (`place-truth-lines.md`); hand lines are W46.place-hands' forced alignment, checked.
  - The owner checked 97 typed places where blla and PP-OCRv6 both disagreed with the truth, and 5 hand pages
    (W46.place-lines-owner-ok, 2026-10-31). Folded here: 50 lines merged where he read one line; the three tables he
    said break at more places than the sheet marked (p024 t002/t003, p057 t007, p066 t010/t011) left out, which is
    every "truth wrong" split, hence 0 splits; p096's two merges applied and line 20 left out.
  - Vision is the scored reader in every arm and drafted half the typed lines. That favours no arm: every arm lays
    the same Vision words, and a line is scored only when Vision read all of its words, so an OCR miss is never a
    selection failure.
- **Selection.** Every placement-truth page with a saved W46.enh-dewarp lattice (58 of the 58). Strata come from
  measured geometry, not capture tags (audit OCR-8): the page's median Vision line angle (skewed ≥ 1°), the
  perspective fit's angle range across the page (≥ 1°) and UVDoc's bend (≥ the median, 7.9 px), from
  `enh-dewarp-pages.csv`. No ABBYY text was used.
- **Comparison.** One Vision reading on the raw image; each arm differs only in the image the layer sits on and the
  map that carries words and truth to it (rotation, the quad homography, the UVDoc lattice after the quad). Same
  drags, same scorer, same PDFKit. Lines an arm's image cuts fail on that arm. Both OCR sets: not applicable — the
  895-page public set has no line truth for selection, and Real5's warp and skew folders (named in the item) have
  no saved Vision reading or lattice in W46.enh-dewarp, so they are not scored here (W46.place-geom-real5, behind
  W46.nice-to-have).
- **Noise.** n = 58 pages, 1,682 scored lines (226 hand). Intervals are paired bootstrap over pages (2,000 draws).
  The run is deterministic: a fresh rerun on 2026-11-01 reproduced every count of the first, so the rerun floor is
  0 and the page sample is the noise. The skewed stratum is 17 pages, so its interval is wide (+3.4 to +14.2).
- **Scope.** PDFKit only (Preview and the apps); Acrobat, Chrome and Safari are W46.place-print's viewer harness. The
  drags start and end on the truth word's ink, and again 0.35 of its height outside it; a real user's drag varies
  more. Loose sheets and the 96's bound pages as photographed; no book gutter page was tested apart. It does not
  settle whether a deskewed copy reads better to the eye, nor why 344 of the 1,682 lines (20%) fail on every arm;
  that is the layer's own placement, W46.place-print's question.
