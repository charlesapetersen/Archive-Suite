## The rule

Every place the owner emptied was one not written in the document, and the two he upheld are written (one in a letterhead address); his note on a third says the place is not given in the text. So the sending place is a place the document writes, in a dateline, a letterhead or an address, and never one inferred from the collection, the content or an institution's name (`ent_truth.place_rule`). A letterhead place stays: the owner upheld one, and his note that a letterhead is not always where a letter was sent from is a caveat with no rule the text can apply. The definition the truth was drafted with (`ent_truth.PROMPT`: "the dateline's place, or the letterhead address") already asked for a written place; what changes is that its inferred classes no longer count, so a route that fills one is wrong when filled, where before it was set aside.

## Measurement card

- **Truth.** W46.fix-ent-truth (GPT 5.6 Luna and Claude Sonnet 5 drafts, Sonnet adjudicating), with the owner's answers on the 23 fields his sheet asked (all of them fields Sonnet ruled for its own reader) and six confirming notes. The rest of the set has no owner check; the 20 asked were chosen as Sonnet's self-rulings, so they say how far to trust those, not the agreed rows. No scored route drafted the truth.
- **Selection.** The 138 correspondence documents and the reports' own subsets (the 82 with cached tagging replies); nothing chosen by a scored route or by ABBYY text here. The sheet's entity controls were drawn by the adjudicator's side, not by a route.
- **Comparison.** Each route's cached replies exactly as its report scored them; only the truth changes. ent-score's tagging replies read ABBYY text (scored before the owner's 2026-10-30 rule; no new ABBYY arm is run here); the Gemini 3.7 arms read Apple Vision text.
- **Noise.** Paired bootstrap intervals above, beside each prompt's rerun floor. A difference inside its interval, or inside the rerun floor, is a tie.
- **Scope.** The rule rests on 12 owner rulings on places and on `place_kind`, a text heuristic that cannot tell a letterhead from a typed address or check a place against the image; its dateline count is low because the OCR often puts the date on its own line. Whether a letterhead place should count when nothing else names the place is the owner's caveat and is not tested. The app's tagging prompt and the dedicated entity prompt still say "the letterhead's or the dateline's city" without forbidding an inferred place; W46.ent-place-rule-prompt measures adding that before the app changes.
