## The review, two-box design (2026-11-04; supersedes the 2026-10-23 one below)

1. **The question, and what it could change.** Does a LoRA continuation of the corpus-trained 2B, trained on a
   user's corrections of the first k gaps of one Lincoln box (k = 50 and 200) plus a replay of corpus windows, make
   fewer errors on the NEXT box of the same material than the 2B does? Yes → the kit's settings offer "adapt the
   segmenter to this collection" once a box is reviewed, and the review screen's corrections feed it. No → the
   settings route a hard new material to a cloud voter and stop promising adaptation. A settings rule either way
   (owner, 2026-11-04).
2. **Why the results in hand do not settle it.** The cheap refit (seg-adapt-curve.md) cuts box-1 held-out errors
   only from 17.7 to about 15 per 100 gaps; the remaining errors are confident ones only retraining can move, and
   nothing has retrained. The single-box LoRA would score on 60 held-out gaps, inside the noise; box 2 gives about
   250 scored gaps that no training row saw.
3. **Size.** Seven jobs: stage (CPU); merge the corpus 2B (CPU, kit-gate's merge-job.sh); two LoRA trainings
   (one 48 GB GPU each, 16 and 62 correction windows with three replay windows for each, one epoch; about 10-30
   minutes each on FarmShare's figures); two LoRA merges (CPU); three scoring runs (base, k50, k200) over box 2's
   window pack and box 1's (about 175 windows each, minutes after a 10-30 minute vLLM start). About 2-3 GPU-hours
   expected; the jobs' time limits cap it at 5.25 (the manifest's ceiling). No training pilot exists: the
   estimate is FarmShare's figure for a few hundred windows, and k=200 is 248 rows (62 + 186 replay) × 2 epochs.
   Smaller would not do: k = 50 alone cannot say whether more corrections help, and the base run must be on the
   same hardware and serving as the LoRAs for a paired comparison.
4. **Verdict: useful now** (owner promotion, 2026-11-04; Phase 4 does not wait on it).

## The review (2026-10-23, single-box design)

1. **The question, and what it could change.** Can a LoRA continuation of the corpus-trained Qwen3.5 2B, trained on
   up to 200 of a user's corrected gaps plus a replay of corpus windows, teach the 2B a material it fails on? If it
   can, the kit promises "LoRA when a few hundred corrections exist" (H §6.3), and the review screen's corrections
   feed it.

2. **Why the results in hand nearly settle it, and what they leave open.**
   - The cheap refit has been measured. On Lincoln the 2B's threshold-and-prior refit cuts held-out errors from 17.7
     to about 15 per 100 gaps at every k from 25 to 200. The errors that remain are confident ones, so only
     retraining or another model could move them; that much is open.
   - The route that settles Lincoln exists already. Claude c1 scores 90.0% there against the 2B's 34.9%, and the 2B
     against a cloud partner reaches 98% after 54.8 checks per 100 pages.
   - The experiment as specified cannot answer the open part well. Lincoln has 260 gaps. At k=200 only 60 held-out
     gaps remain, about three or four documents, and pages exact at k=200 is already 82.7-87.0% from the corrected
     gaps alone with no refit. Any LoRA gain would sit inside that noise. Stanton's errors are the 2B's over-splits
     on a box of the owner's own kind, where the teacher already does better (81.9%).
   - The question the kit actually needs is different: whether corrections from one box of a new material carry over
     to the next box of the same material. That needs at least two boxes of the new material. The bench has one
     (Lincoln).

3. **Size.**
   - The arm as filed: 2 boxes × k ∈ {50, 200}, one LoRA run each (one L40S-class GPU, about 10-30 minutes on
     FarmShare's figures for a few hundred windows with replay), plus scoring each merged model with vLLM over that
     box's held-out windows (about 5 minutes).
   - Estimate: about 2-3 GPU-hours, plus one CPU job to build the torch and peft overlay over the vLLM image.
   - Nothing for it exists on Sherlock yet:
     - no training venv (FarmShare's `train-venv` was not copied);
     - no merged 2B (only the adapter is in the FarmShare copy, per `execution-plans/sherlock-access.md`);
     - no job script.
   - The build is mostly Mac-side and wants a session of its own:
     - target replies built from the truth for each labelled window;
     - the prefix, held-out and replay split;
     - the scorer.
   - A smaller version, Lincoln at k=200 only, would answer the same narrow question and would be just as noisy.

4. **Verdict: nice-to-have.** This changes no app route, default or recommendation now. The review screen's
   selection rule, the laptop segmenter (not doc-split-v1) and the cheap refit's value are settled without it.
   Lincoln-like material routes to a cloud voter. The right form of the test, with two or more boxes of one new
   material and corrections from the first scored on the second, needs data the bench does not have. If the owner
   opens `W46.nice-to-have`, start by finding a second box of a Lincoln-like material (LoC's other Lincoln Papers
   series would do, as public-segmentation.md's process). Do not run the single-box version.
