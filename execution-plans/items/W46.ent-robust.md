## Necessity and size review (2026-10-29)

1. **The question, and what it could change.** At what page CER the entity fields should switch off, by route,
   for the extractor the app would use: the local LLM through a prompt, not a tagger (W46.ent-score found no
   local tagger usable, ed2 F1 0.24 at best). The taggers' numbers measure robustness of a tagger, and the 27B
   arm measures it for an LLM extractor, which is the one the app ships: it sets the switch-off rule the
   report gives for the API and 27B routes, and whether the Mac route's Vision text is good enough to extract
   from. The G17 arm puts the 27B beside the published Business Letters median (0.55), the one external anchor.
2. **Why the results in hand do not settle it.** The taggers degrade on noise, but they also miss most names on
   clean text, so their curve need not be an LLM's: an LLM corrects garbled names (ent-score: 13 of the 27B's
   39 nowhere-names were corrections). No LLM has been run on route text against truth text anywhere.
3. **Size.** Two jobs: a CPU stage (copy, hash check, model fetch if absent) and one GPU job.
   - The entity arm: 2,078 text-only requests (the 96: truth and five routes, 633 with 64 post-corrected
     readings; the public Latin pages: truth and four routes, 1,445 with 2; failed readings are not sent),
     4.7 M prompt characters (about
     1.3 M tokens), replies of about 100 tokens.
   - The G17 arm: 57 letters, all pages in one request each (98 scans).
   - Estimate from W46.ocr-postcorrect's Sherlock run of the same server (job 49139318: 458 requests, about
     300 reply tokens each, 1,339 s at concurrency 8): at concurrency 32 and a third of the reply length,
     about 20-30 minutes of generation, plus up to 30 minutes of start-up. About 1 GPU-hour; ceiling 2 hours
     (the job's limit).
   - Each arm is its own pilot: 8 requests first, and the full arm only if 6 stop cleanly. The arms are
     independent: a failed pilot in one does not stop the other.
   - Smaller would not do much: the 96 alone is 633 requests, and the both-sets rule (CLAUDE.md) asks for
     the public set too; the public half is 1,445 short requests, about 15 minutes. Dropping it saves a
     quarter of an hour of a job that has to start anyway.
4. **Verdict: useful now.** It sets the switch-off rule for entities by route, which the app needs before
   showing the fields from the Mac route, at about 1 GPU-hour.

## The pack

`~/.local/state/archive-autonomous/artifacts/ent-robust-20261029/`: `ent/requests.jsonl`, `g17/requests.jsonl`
and `g17/images/` (the benchmark's 98 JPEGs, unchanged), `support/` (`postcorrect_cluster.py`,
`check_pack.py`) and `support/job/` (`submit.sh`, `stage-job.sh`, `job.sh`, `sh.env`, `bin/`), `freeze.json`
(every file's SHA-256). Copy it to `$GROUP_HOME/$USER/archive-suite/ent-robust-20261029`, then:

    bash "$GROUP_HOME/$USER/archive-suite/ent-robust-20261029/support/job/submit.sh" "$GROUP_HOME/$USER/archive-suite/ent-robust-20261029"

Dry run on the Mac (done, passes): `ARCHIVE_UNATTENDED=1 bash <pack>/support/job/submit.sh <pack> --dry-run`.
Results land in `$GROUP_HOME/$USER/archive-suite/sherlock-results/ent-robust-20261029/{ent,g17}/out/`; bring
them home to `~/Library/Caches/ArchiveSuiteRehearsal/sherlock-results/ent-robust-20261029/`. Then the next
engine session runs `python3 ent_g17.py score --root <that>/g17 --label qwen3.6-27b` and
`python3 ent_robust.py report`, writes the 27B rows into the Reading, and ships the item.

The model is fetched at `main` (Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B-FP8, the checkpoint every bench 27B row used); the job
records the snapshot hash in `runtime/<job>.json`.
