# Model inventory for the Archive Suite processing review

As of 2026-10-20. Built from pages fetched on 2026-10-20: the Hugging Face API (api/models search and per-model records), model cards and LICENSE files, arXiv abstracts, GitHub READMEs and release pages, and Apple developer documentation JSON.

## How to read this file

- URL rule. For every Hugging Face row the URL is `https://huggingface.co/` plus the repo id in the first column. Non-Hugging-Face sources are written out in full.
- TESTED means the model is on the owner's already-tested list. Those rows are short.
- NEW means released or last materially updated after 2026-06-01. The brief said "newer than 2026-06 especially".
- "Released" is the repo's createdAt date from the HF API. Where the card says a newer checkpoint replaced the old one inside the same repo, that is noted.
- Params come from the HF API `safetensors.total` field (total, including embeddings and vision tower) unless a card says otherwise. For MoE models the total is given, with active parameters where the card states them.
- "HF dl" is the API `downloads` field at fetch time (a recent-window count, not all-time). "likes" is the HF like count. Both are adoption signals only. Repos whose weights are not served as transformers or safetensors (for example DocLayout-YOLO, ONNX-only repos) show near-zero numbers that mean nothing.
- Memory columns are weights only, approximate, inferred (marked [I]) as params x 0.56 GB (4-bit) and x 1.06 GB (8-bit). Add 1 to 3 GB for KV cache, vision activations and runtime. Where a repo states a measured size, that figure is used and marked [M].
- Benchmark figures are vendor-reported unless the row says otherwise. Benchmark versions differ (OmniDocBench v1.5 and v1.6 are different test sets) and scores across versions must not be compared. None of these benchmarks contains photographed archival boxes or handwritten 20th-century material, so they are a weak guide to our task.
- Mac column: MLX means a repo exists under mlx-community or a named community repo (the exact repo is given); mlx-vlm v0.7.9 (released 2026-10-19) lists model support at https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm. For llama.cpp the list used is https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md (fetched 2026-10-20); it names OCR support for PaddleOCR-VL, GLM-OCR, DeepSeek-OCR, dots.ocr and HunyuanOCR, and pre-quantized vision models for Gemma 4 (E2B, E4B, 26B-A4B, 31B), Qwen2/2.5-VL, InternVL 2.5/3, SmolVLM, Pixtral, Mistral Small 3.1 and Llama 4 Scout. A model absent from that list can still have community GGUF files that work. Core ML means a Core ML package exists.
- Languages are what the card claims, not what was measured.

## 8. Not tested and worth testing, with reasons

Ordered by how much each would change a decision. Reasons are for our task: phone photographs of archival boxes, mixed print and handwriting, segmentation into items, dating, and a range from laptop-only users to clusters.

1. Baidu Unlimited-OCR (baidu/Unlimited-OCR, MIT, 3.3B total with about 0.5B active). It reads up to 16 pages in one generation and emits layout boxes, and a measured MLX build runs at roughly 200 to 260 tokens/s on a 24 GB M5 Pro. That makes it the one OCR model that fits the laptop tier, outputs boxes, and sees multiple pages at once, which bears on splitting. Its 1.98M downloads make it the most widely used 2026 OCR release.
2. TeleOCR (XingChen-AGI/TeleOCR, Apache-2.0, 1.4B). It is trained for camera-captured and curved pages as well as digital ones, which is our input. It claims the top OmniDocBench v1.6 score (96.87) in its own table and a lead on the Dr.DocBench challenge. No MLX build, only GGUF, so a Mac test goes through llama.cpp.
3. Xiaomi-OCR-0 (SeerRay-Lab/Xiaomi-OCR-0, Apache-2.0, 0.87B). Claims first place on Real5-OmniDocBench (scanned, warped, photographed, skewed) and a score on historical handwriting and calligraphy (Chronicles-OCR). Smallest model with a claim about both photographed pages and handwriting. Released 2026-09-29, so almost no outside evidence yet (7.8k downloads).
4. OvisOCR2 (ATH-MaaS/OvisOCR2, Apache-2.0, 0.85B). Has MLX 4, 6 and 8-bit builds and the second-highest OmniDocBench v1.6 score (96.58) among sub-1B models. Easy to run on a laptop.
5. Qwen3.8-27B (Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B, Apache-2.0). We tested Qwen3.6-27B. The card reports OmniDocBench 1.5 of 91.1 against 89.4 for 3.6. It has an MLX 4-bit build with 885k downloads and is the natural replacement for the general-purpose segmentation and dating role.
6. MiniCPM-V 4.7 1B and 35B-A3B (openbmb, Apache-2.0, 2026-10). The 1B (about 0.9 GB at 4-bit) is the smallest general VLM here with a fresh MLX port; the 35B-A3B is a MoE (about 3B active) that the card says is designed to run on devices with expert prefetching. Both are untested for dating and box segmentation, and the card has no document figures.
7. Chandra-2 (datalab-to/chandra-ocr-2, 5.3B). Highest handwriting claim among models with an MLX build (olmOCR-bench 85.8, "excellent handwriting support"). The licence blocks commercial use above US$2M revenue or funding and blocks competing products, so it could be tested but probably not bundled. Surya-2 (we tested partly) has the same licence form at US$5M.
8. Infinity-Parser2-Pro (infly/Infinity-Parser2-Pro, Apache-2.0, 35B) reports the highest olmOCR-bench figure found (87.6%). For the cluster tier only, since 35B has no MLX build and needs about 20 GB even at 4-bit. The Flash variant (2.2B) is the laptop-sized sibling.
9. Nemotron-Parse 2.0 (nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-Parse-2.0, 0.9B, OpenMDW) and Nemotron-3.5-Parse (0.63B). Output includes layout class, box and reading order together with text, and 2.0 has MLX 4 and 8-bit builds. The card claims improved handwriting extraction. Permissive licence.
10. HunyuanOCR-1.5 (tencent/HunyuanOCR, 1.1B). Card says it was trained with historical OCR and ancient-script data and supports 4K images. Licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea, so test only if the user base is acceptable. llama.cpp GGUF exists, MLX is community only.
11. Datalab Indra OCR (datalab-to/indra-ocr, 1M parameters, CPU ONNX). It sets the floor for a laptop with no GPU: boxes, reading order, any page angle, about 210 ms per page on 4 cores. Its own card says handwriting is weak, so it can serve as a line detector and printed-text reader, not an HTR model.
12. PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (we tested 1.5). Same architecture with a 1.5-to-1.6 jump on its own benchmarks (94.87 to 96.33 on v1.6); a drop-in upgrade with official GGUF and MLX builds.
13. Page-stream segmentation by VLM windowing (arXiv 2609.22620 plus the kaseris/turbopss harness). No pretrained splitter exists, so splitting is a prompt-and-window problem over a general VLM. The paper reports a window size beyond which accuracy drops sharply, which we would need to find for our own material.
14. Apple Vision `DetectDocumentSegmentationRequest` and `RecognizeDocumentsRequest` as the zero-install baseline for page finding and for a printed-text floor. Cheap to run and the natural reference for the laptop tier.
15. MonkeyOCRv2-B-Parsing (zenosai, Apache-2.0, 0.88B), claims 17 languages and photographed documents and has a CPU path. Lower priority: no MLX build, and TeleOCR and Xiaomi-OCR-0 make the same claims.
16. For historical handwriting specifically: the third-party NARA fine-tune card shows a fine-tune of LightOnOCR-3-4B was no better in aggregate than the stock model (CER 0.2312 against 0.2057 on 762 held-out Revolutionary War pension pages), so the stock LightOnOCR-3-4B we already tested remains the reference for that kind of material.

## 9. Gaps and caveats

- No benchmark in this file uses photographs of archival boxes or 20th-century handwriting. The OmniDocBench v1.5 and v1.6 figures are for printed documents (v1.6 is a different test set from v1.5), olmOCR-bench is mostly English PDFs, and the Real5 set is the nearest to phone photography.
- Many cards put their benchmark tables in images, so figures for LightOnOCR-3, HunyuanOCR-1.5 (own card), PaddleOCR-VL-1.6's Real5 table and others could not be read from text. Where a figure comes from another model's card it is labelled third party.
- Memory estimates are formulas, not measurements, except where marked [M]. They omit the KV cache, which for long multi-page outputs (13,420 tokens for 14 pages with Unlimited-OCR) is not small.
- The Vision framework's supported recognition languages, accuracy levels and handwriting behaviour were not in the fetched JSON, and the Foundation Models image-input platform version and limits were not stated.
- Licences were read from LICENSE files for: Chandra-2, Surya-2, HunyuanOCR, Youtu-Parsing, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, LFM2.5-VL-3B, CHURRO-3B (Qwen Research), LensVLM (Apple). Others rely on the HF licence field and the card's licence line. Logics-Parsing-V3 states none. Nanonets-OCR2-3B's HF record has no licence field.
- Ling 3 Flash VL and the Qwen3.5 / Qwen3-VL variants were not re-fetched because they are already tested.
- Searches covered Hugging Face (search and author listings sorted by downloads and by creation date), GitHub repository search, and arXiv. Models released only on other hubs, or only as hosted APIs, were not searched for.
