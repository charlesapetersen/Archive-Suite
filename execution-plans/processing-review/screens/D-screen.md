## 3. READ shortlist (ranked)

1. **DvD (D14) with its AnyPhotoDoc6300 benchmark.** It dewarps by denoising coordinates, so no pixel is invented; it takes inputs up to 2000x3000, close to our 2400 px; and it supplies real photo pairs and an MLLM readability metric. Test: run it (when the code is out; the abstract says "will be available") on 50 of our pages against UVDoc (D04) and DocRes dewarp (D07); score with OCR text CER against our own transcriptions, and with a pixel-difference check that no mark disappears. Watch the CC BY-NC-ND licence on the paper and the unstated code licence.
2. **UVDoc / PaddleOCR UVDoc (D04, D05).** MIT/Apache, small, ships weights, easy on a Mac. It is the baseline dewarper. Test: dewarp 50 curved phone pages; look specifically for cropped margins (the issue in D05), and for whether the 2D grid can be exported so an OCR layer made on the dewarped page can be mapped back.
3. **DocRes (D07).** One MIT toolbox with six tasks. Test each task separately on pages with stamps, pencil annotations and faint marks: dewarp, deshadow and appearance are the candidates for the reading copy, binarisation is not. Record which marks vanish per task, since the paper reports only restoration scores.
4. **Competition on Binarizing Photographed Document Images 2024 (D30), with D31.** Phone photographs with ground truth. Test: obtain the dataset, run the classical and learned binarisers, and use it to judge our own deskew and contrast steps on phone images.
5. **Printed/handwritten segmentation with overlap (D40, SignaTR6K), plus the marginalia pipeline (D43).** This is the detection route: mark where handwriting and stamps are, never erase them. Test: on 100 of our pages with annotations, label handwriting regions by hand, then see whether a model trained on SignaTR6K or a VLM prompted with the page finds them; record recall and the overlap case.
6. **PreP-OCR (D33) together with TAIR (D34).** One shows a large real-page gain from restoration, the other names the hallucination failure and a mitigation. Test: take a restoration model, run it on our pages, and apply D34's idea (compare OCR before and after on spans that the restored version "adds") plus D33's idea (several passes, median fusion) to see whether any text appears only after enhancement.
7. **eScriptorium enhancement study (D32).** The humanities measurement of whether binarisation or deblurring helps recognition. Test: replicate its design (same HTR engine, raw versus enhanced inputs) on 100 of our pages with a modern recogniser, to see if enhancement matters at all for a strong reader.
8. **TeleOCR (D22) and DocAligner (D21).** TeleOCR reports that dewarping helps pipelines more than end-to-end VLMs, which addresses the staged-versus-one-shot question; DocAligner gives a photo-to-clean registration that could carry an OCR layer between the original and rectified images. Test: run a frontier VLM on raw versus dewarped pages and compare CER; try DocAligner (if code exists) for mapping.

Apple Vision (D53) is the cheap baseline for crop/deskew and should be tested alongside the shortlist, though it does not fit the cap as a paper.

## 5. Findings by the owner's four concerns

**(1) Which methods remove content and which only correct geometry or contrast.**
- Geometry only: UVDoc, DocTr/DocTr++, DocGeoNet, DocScanner, DewarpNet, D2Dewarp, AADD, Apple's perspective correction. They output a mapping and resample the page; they do not paint new content, though resampling blurs and cropping can lose edge text (the UVDoc cropping issue).
- DvD is generative but over coordinates, so it also stays in this class.
- Contrast/illumination: GCDRNet (a multiplicative shading map) and the deshadow and appearance tasks of DocRes. Content-preserving in design, but not verified for faint marks.
- Removes content by design: the Rényi DIE ("scribbles", bleed-through), DocDiff (seals, watermarks), Uni-DocDiff (handwriting removal), DE-GAN (watermarks), Engin's stamp cleaner, all bleed-through removers, and every binariser (it discards faint ink and colour, so a pencil note can vanish). DocRes is mixed: its binarisation and appearance tasks belong here.
- Rule for us: the reading copy gets only geometry and, if tested, shading correction. Enhancement that removes or rewrites marks is for a separate OCR-input copy.

**(2) Hallucination in enhancement models and how it is measured.** TAIR names "text-image hallucination" in diffusion restoration (scene text). GLYPH-SR argues PSNR/SSIM miss character-level errors and uses OCR F1. PreP-OCR uses median patch fusion because hallucinations are rarely consistent across passes, and found GPT-4o's mistakes harder to correct than Tesseract's. HalluGen inserts controlled hallucinations to test detectors (medical). For super-resolution, one ultra-low-resolution paper warned a super-resolved image would likely hallucinate characters. I found no published text-specific, reference-free hallucination metric for document restoration. A usable test of our own: OCR the raw and enhanced image and flag any word present only in the enhanced one; and compare on a page whose text we have already transcribed.

**(3) Detection, separation, transcription of annotations, stamps, signatures, marginalia.** Pixel-level printed/handwritten separation with overlap exists (D40, ICCV 2023, data by form, no code seen). Marginalia detect-and-transcribe exists for early books (D43, code released). Signature and stamp detectors and data are plentiful but sit on modern scanned or synthetic documents (D47-D49). The Hugging Face signature detector blog (https://huggingface.co/blog/samuellimabraz/signature-detection-model, search) merges Tobacco800 with another set. I found no standard marginalia-detection benchmark and no dataset of annotated 20th-century typed pages. Recent VLMs were not tested in the papers I found for this; that comparison is open.

**(4) Mapping dewarped geometry back to the original.** UVDoc outputs a 2D unwarping grid (from the paper abstract) and the older DocUNet approach estimated a forward map then a backward map; control-point methods (D20) build a dense backward map by thin-plate splines. I did not find a paper that states how to put an OCR text layer back on the original photo, and the UVDoc README says nothing on exposing the grid. DocAligner (D21) and DocMatcher (D18) are the closest registration methods. In practice this is possible whenever the model gives a sampling grid, and has to be verified per model.

## 6. Open questions for the deep read

1. Does the UVDoc grid (or DvD's coordinate map) export at full resolution, and can it be inverted to carry OCR boxes to the original photo with sub-line accuracy?
2. Does UVDoc/PaddleOCR crop margins on phone photos, as issue #15717 reports? What happens to 2400 px input (internal resize, then upsample)?
3. Which DocRes tasks, if any, drop faint pencil, stamp ink or bleed-through? Does the Rényi demo erase marks we want?
4. Is the DvD code released, and what is its licence? The paper licence is CC BY-NC-ND; ask whether the weights are usable.
5. How much does dewarping help a frontier VLM (as opposed to a two-stage pipeline) on our pages? TeleOCR says less for end-to-end models; verify.
6. Does enhancement help a modern recogniser at all (D32 tested only Kraken on one corpus)?
7. Can a general VLM find handwriting, stamps and signatures on typed pages well enough to flag them (with boxes) without a trained detector?
8. Is the 2024 photographed-binarisation dataset downloadable, and under what licence?
9. Which Apple Vision output do we get (corners only, or a corrected image), and does it handle curvature?
10. Official DIBCO 2023-2025 results and the Semantic Scholar forward citations of DocRes, UVDoc and DvD were not searched here and should be.
