## 3. READ shortlist (ranked)

1. **A04 Multi-Split Boundary Decision (2609.22620).** It is the only paper that measures how window size interacts with model and corpus for zero-shot LLM splitting. We saw 87-93% on our boxes with 7-page windows and 35-47% on Lincoln; this paper says the best window moves between corpora (K=5-10 on TABME++, K=5-7 on VRDU) and recommends tuning it on a small labeled set. Test: on Lincoln, sweep window size (3, 5, 7, 10) and the one-boundary variant on 50-100 hand-labeled pages and see whether the Lincoln loss is a window effect or a prompt/model effect. Also check whether text input beats image input on Lincoln, as the paper reports for Gemini.
2. **A05 Heidenreich et al., LLMs for PSS (2408.11981 / COLING 2025).** Fine-tuned 7B decoder gets doc F1 0.967 on TABME++ against 0.62-0.64 for encoders and XGBoost. It supports our Qwen3.5 2B/4B students (79% on locked test boxes) and says OCR quality drives the result. Test: re-run our student on TABME++ test as a sanity anchor, and compare our OCR (Vision/ABBYY) to the Microsoft-OCR gap they report. Ask whether their straight-through-processing rate is a better user-facing score than page accuracy.
3. **A20 doc-split-v1.** An open, small, ONNX model with CRF smoothing and calibrated scores. Its own card shows 0.936 on its in-distribution synthetic set but 0.704 on TABME++ and 0.820 on Tobacco800 zero-shot, the same cross-collection drop we see. Test: run it on the locked test boxes and on Lincoln unmodified and with a CRF/threshold retuned on 50 Lincoln pages. It is a ready laptop-profile component.
4. **A09 + A10 OpenPSS (TPDL 2024) and the HF mirror.** The only large, real, non-synthetic, heterogeneous page-image set with a train/test split and a cross-dataset result. The nutrient card reports cloud VLMs at 0.244 boundary F1 on OpenPSS-long, which would be a strong warning for prompt-only approaches. Test: run our windowed VLM prompt and our students on OpenPSS-long and SHORT (SHORT has few boundaries) to see whether the Lincoln drop reproduces on a public set. Note the Dutch language and the license caveat.
5. **A01 DocSplit (2602.15958) and its metrics.** Packet score, clustering score (V-measure + Rand), Kendall tau and three strictness levels of page accuracy give a more graded scoreboard than "exactly right document." Test: score our boxes with the clustering metric and Kendall tau, and see whether the 87-93% and 35-47% figures change shape (partial credit for near-correct splits). Treat the dataset as synthetic and non-commercial.
6. **A22 doc-split-benchmark with score.py.** It supplies boundary F1, Cohen's kappa, WindowDiff and Pk in one scorer, with the argument that F1 alone is gameable at sparse boundary rates. Test: report kappa and WindowDiff for our own results; Lincoln's boundary rate probably differs from our own boxes and F1 may be hiding that.
7. **A06 TABME++ (with A07 TABME).** The largest labeled set of 20th-century US typed business papers (Tobacco Truth archive), MIT licence, images and OCR included. Use it as training data for a student and as a zero-shot test of the students' transfer. The TABME drag-and-drop metric is the one published correction-cost measure.
8. **A26 zbMATH document separation (2604.00554).** A collection-scale job (810,977 documents) using fine-tuned LLMs with majority voting, reaching 89.1% boundary accuracy. Test: apply majority voting over several window offsets or prompts to Lincoln; if errors are uncorrelated this is cheap accuracy for premium and API profiles.

Not shortlisted but worth one sitting: A12 (clustering with a known document count) for the label-free route, and A49 for the correction-loop design.

## 5. Open questions for the deep read

1. Why does windowed prompting fall from 87-93% to 35-47% on Lincoln? A04 gives a window-size and text-versus-image explanation; A09 and A20 give a distribution-shift explanation. Which one the Lincoln data supports decides whether we tune K, retrain, or add a calibration layer.
2. How many labeled Lincoln pages does adaptation need? None of the papers measures learning curves for new collections. A04 uses a small validation set to pick K; A25 tunes configuration against a small labeled set; A20 ships CRF and calibration that could be refit on few labels. Compare these on 25, 50, 100, 200 pages.
3. Does a label-free signal help? A12 (agglomerative clustering with a known document count) and A11 (dossier metadata) are the only candidates. Is a finding-aid folder or item count available for Lincoln?
4. What does correction learning look like in practice? I found no paper on active learning for PSS. The usable pieces are TABME's drag-and-drop metric, ConfBench-style confidence routing, and the shredded-document recommender (A49). The deep read should test whether per-page confidence from a student model ranks errors well enough to put the 10% least certain pages in front of the user.
5. Which metric should the project report? Page-in-exact-document accuracy, boundary F1, kappa, WindowDiff, Pk, packet score and panoptic quality disagree on near misses. Compute all on the locked test boxes and see which one tracks what a user sees as effort.
6. Does a graph or clustering formulation help over per-page boundary labels when documents are not contiguous (misfiled pages, interleaved letters)? DocSplit's mono/poly/interleaved settings are the only measurement; box photographs are mostly contiguous but not always.
7. How much of the gap between our students (79%) and the 0.967 doc F1 of A05 is OCR quality, base-model size or task difficulty? Run the student on TABME++.
8. HR-DocStream: ask the owner for the source. If it exists it is not indexed anywhere I could search.
9. Licences for shipping: DocSplit is non-commercial; LayoutLMv3 weights carry a non-commercial paper licence; TABME's licence is unstated; doc-split-v1 is Apache-2.0 and TABME++ is MIT. For the Archive Suite's other users, only those two are clearly shippable.
10. Citation counts are low (the whole DocSplit and OpenPSS citing set is under ten items each), so claims from industry preprints (A04, A14, A20, A26) deserve a replication on our material before being relied on.
