# Strand G screen: named entities and correspondence metadata

Date of search: 2026-10-20. Scope: NER on noisy historical text, LLM extraction of sender / recipient / date / place from letters, entity linking with time ranges, KIE from forms and letters, and use of entities to date or group documents.

## 1. Search log

Semantic Scholar's citations endpoint and arXiv listing pages were not queried directly, so forward citation chains from the seed papers were not followed; this is the main gap in the screen. Fetched (F): Zenodo 18862770, GitHub RISE-UNIBAS humanities_data_benchmark and its issue 117, arXiv 2504.00414, 2508.18090, 2511.10404, 1301.2405. The CIKM index-card paper returned 403 (ACM) and a binary PDF (KIT); its details are from the search summary only.

Two findings from the search itself. First, I found no paper that dates documents from their named entities with a modern method; the entity-as-time-proxy idea appears only in a 2006 paper and in secondary summaries. Second, I found no peer-reviewed benchmark of sender / recipient / date / place extraction from letters; the nearest are a DH conference talk and an open benchmark issue.

## 3. READ shortlist

1. G16, Strutz and Scholger, letter metadata pipeline. It is the only study of exactly our optional feature: sender, date and place from letters, with commercial vs local models and authority-file reconciliation. Test: take about 100 letters from our boxes, stratified by typed vs handwritten, dateline present vs absent, and run date / place / sender / recipient extraction with one local model and one API model, scoring each field against hand labels. Read the slides for per-field figures that the Zenodo page omits.
2. G17, with G18, the humanities_data_benchmark. It is a running harness with a letters task (send date, sender, receiver) and card and minutes tasks that resemble ours, under GPL code and CC BY ground truth. Test: run its Business Letters task unchanged with our candidate models to calibrate, then add a task built from our own letters in the same format, with G18's three-way split of named, role-inferred and correspondence-inferred persons.
3. G25, CIKM archival-records extraction. It compares a fine-tuned small OCR-free model (Donut) against multimodal LLMs on a small archival set, which is the staged-vs-one-shot question at field level. Test: fine-tune a small extractor on 300 of our labelled forms and compare it with a zero-shot VLM; record where each wins by field type.
4. G13, Greif et al., multimodal LLMs for OCR, post-correction and NER. It shows a two-step use of a VLM (transcribe, then correct, then structure) that reaches under 1% CER on directories. Test: apply its post-correction step to our OCR output and measure whether entity extraction improves downstream.
5. G35, GLiNER. It is the cheap, local, label-flexible extractor for the laptop profile. Test: zero-shot person / organisation / place / date on OCR of typed memos at a range of CER, against an LLM prompt on the same text; measure the loss as noise rises.
6. G04, Ehrmann et al. survey. It gives the failure modes (noise, small data, tagset drift) and the resource list before we design any annotation. Read for the evaluation protocol and the noise findings, then reuse its metric choices (strict vs fuzzy span match).
7. G37, Temporal Sequencing of Documents. It orders undated documents from word-usage drift with no dated training set, and a box of papers is often a mix of dated and undated items. Test: on one box where most letters carry datelines, hide the dates and see whether the ordering recovers them.
8. G36, Dating medieval English charters. It is the base method (kNN and prevalence on shingles, median error 6 years with dated training data). Test: use our dated documents as the training set and predict held-out dates from n-gram prevalence, as a text-only baseline against a VLM asked for the year.

## 5. Open questions for the deep read

1. What are the per-field accuracies in G16 (date, place, sender, recipient), split by presence of a dateline and by local vs API model? Is any code or prompt published?
2. Does any study use extracted entities, not word counts, to date or group documents? The only lead is a 2006 claim (G38) and secondary summaries; the original needs checking. If not, entities may be useful as segmentation cues (a new sender, a new letterhead, a new date line) rather than as dating features.
3. How much does OCR error change entity extraction on typed material at 1-3% CER versus handwriting at 5-7% CER (G08, G47)? Is there a CER above which the optional metadata feature should be switched off?
4. In G25, does a small fine-tuned extractor beat a zero-shot VLM on archival forms, and at what training-set size?
5. Can the app's existing 2-6 subject tags be extended in the same call to emit sender / recipient / date / place, or does a separate pass lower accuracy? Neither source tested joint prompts.
6. For place linking, is the World Historical Gazetteer reconciliation adequate for US 20th-century places, or is plain GeoNames sufficient? Time ranges matter little for 20th-century US place names, and Wikidata person linking is mostly useful for public figures.
7. What do the licences allow? G28 is non-commercial; HIPE data is non-commercial share-alike; G16 is CC BY 4.0; G17 mixes GPL and CC BY.
8. Forward citations of G01, G04, G16, G25 and G37 were not followed here; the deep read should run them through Semantic Scholar.
