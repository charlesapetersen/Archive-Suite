## 3. READ shortlist (ranked)

1. **Real5-OmniDocBench (11) with PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 (10).** It is the only benchmark with paired digital and photographed pages. Test: run our candidate stages (DocLayout-YOLO + GLM-OCR, PP-DocLayoutV3 + GLM-OCR, one-shot VLMs) on the Real5 subsets and compare the drop from the scanned subset to warp, screen-photo, illumination and skew. This separates photo damage from layout difficulty without any labelling by us.
2. **PP-DocLayoutV3 / RT-DocLayout (3, 2).** It is the layout stage GLM-OCR was built with, outputs polygons and order, and targets skewed pages. Test: on the 15 newspaper pages, swap DocLayout-YOLO for PP-DocLayoutV3 and keep GLM-OCR fixed; then run both on a set of ordinary letters, memos and forms and count missed regions, merged regions and wrong order.
3. **Historical reading-order benchmark (17) with Orli (18).** Both say order, not detection, is the failure on historical pages, and Orli is trained on archival material. Test: take 30 of our archive photographs with annotations, margin notes and stamps; score region-level order (Kendall tau) for detector boxes plus column clustering, PP-DocLayoutV3's own order, Orli, and a VLM's output order. Check whether the column-clustering result (0.50 to 0.99) holds off newspapers and on pages with annotations.
4. **Historical postcards evaluation (37).** The only published layout-first versus whole-page VLM test on non-newspaper archival material (hybrid wins, VLMs fail on layout but not on single zones). Read the full chapter for how zones were defined and scored; replicate on our pages with crops versus whole page for one fixed VLM.
5. **OmniDocBench (13).** Reports layout mAP and reading-order edit distance by page type, including the newspaper pages that defeat whole-page VLMs. Use its newspaper and notes subsets as a public cross-check of our 0.97 versus 0.2-0.82 finding, and its metric code for order.
6. **American Stories (24) with the Harvard newspaper pipeline (39).** Both are layout-first at scale on newspapers with published measurements, one with detection mAP and article linking, the other with crop-level VLM OCR on workstation hardware and an explicit hallucination caveat. Test: reuse their layout classes and article-linking rule for the newspaper pages; check whether crop OCR hallucinates on blurred crops from phone photographs.
7. **Surya 2 (34) and dots.ocr (9) as joint models.** They are the strongest versions of the opposing design (one model does layout, order and text). Test: run both on the same 15 newspaper pages and the letter set; compare with the two-stage result, and record weight licence terms for redistribution to other users.
8. **Docling heron (5) and MinerU2.5 (8).** Heron is an open layout detector with published CPU and Apple GPU timings, relevant to the laptop-only tier; MinerU2.5 shows the same layout-then-crop split inside one 1.2B model. Test heron as a CPU-only layout stage; read MinerU2.5 for how much the low-resolution layout pass loses.

## 5. Open questions for the deep read

1. Does layout-first help on ordinary archive photographs? I found no study that isolates it for typed letters, memos or forms. The postcards chapter (37) is the closest, and Harvard's newspaper work (39) reports the benefit only on dense newspapers. Newspapers may be the exceptional case, since the OmniDocBench authors note whole-page VLMs fail specifically there. A single-column letter may gain nothing, and a detector could add errors by splitting a letterhead, date and body.
2. How much of the 0.97 versus 0.2-0.82 gap is layout and how much is image resolution? Whole-page VLMs downscale dense pages; MinerU2.5's design (low-resolution layout, native-resolution crops) suggests resolution is a large part. A tiling control (whole page cut into overlapping tiles, no detector) would separate the two.
3. Does DocLayout-YOLO generalise to handwritten annotations, stamps, paper clips, torn edges, adhesive-pasted clippings and photographs with background table? None of the detectors' training sets (DocLayNet, DocSynth, DocStructBench) contain these classes, and Real5 uses only modern documents.
4. Which order method for annotated pages: detector order, PP-DocLayoutV3's built-in order, Orli, column clustering on boxes, or a VLM's own order? The numbers in row 17 are from one informal repository and need replication. Is a permutation even the right target for a page with marginal notes (row 16)?
5. Which Surya weight terms and Nemotron licence terms allow redistribution inside our apps for other users, and which layout models run acceptably on a laptop with no GPU (heron CPU timings in 5, PP-DocLayoutV3 at 33M parameters)?
6. Do layout crops raise hallucination risk when a crop is blurry or empty (Harvard reports invented text on blurry crops, row 39)? What should the pipeline do with low-confidence or empty crops?
7. Does the Orli licence permit our use, and does it work zero-shot on 20th-century typed pages with carbon-copy and stencil text? The abstract names no such material.
8. What metric to use: CER and WER hide order errors (rows 41, 17). A paired set of region-level order score and article-level text accuracy is needed before the staged versus one-shot comparison means anything.
