## 3. READ shortlist (ranked)

1. B1, Temporal Text Classification with LLMs. Its zero-shot, few-shot and fine-tune comparison mirrors our Gemini against fine-tuned Qwen result, on text alone. Test: run its prompt design on OCR text of our unprinted-year documents and see whether text-only Gemini reproduces our 63.4% or whether the image adds to it. That separates textual from visual cues.
2. B46, FermiEval (with B47). Our small models never flag uncertainty and frontier models give a point year. Test: ask each model for 10th and 90th percentile years, then fit a split-conformal correction on a held-out calibration folder. Report coverage per document type and per printed/unprinted year. Check the exchangeability caveat, since calibration must come from the same archive.
3. B2, Evidential deep regression for dating. A Normal-Inverse-Gamma head gives a distribution with aleatoric and epistemic parts in one pass. Test: bolt an NIG head on a Qwen 2B/4B hidden-state pooling and compare coverage and sharpness to B46's conformal wrapper. Treat its 5.4-year MAE with suspicion: three codices, patch-level split.
4. B3, Textlines in Historical Document Classification. The interval Huber loss fits archival dates that arrive as ranges ("1943-1945", "circa 1950"). Test: fine-tune with an interval loss on our date labels and measure the change in exact-year and within-two-years scores on unprinted years against plain cross-entropy over year tokens.
5. B7, Temporal Sequencing of Documents. Folder order is a strong cue, and this is the only unsupervised ordering method I found. Test: take documents in a box in true order, shuffle, recover the order from text drift, then use neighbours' dates as priors for unprinted ones. Compare with giving the VLM the two neighbouring documents' dates and text in the prompt.
6. B37, Generic Image Retrieval for Date Estimation. Learns a period ordering, then dates by neighbours. Test: use its retrieval-by-period idea with our own labelled documents as the reference set, so a new document's year is read from its nearest dated neighbours (letterhead, typeface, paper tone).
7. B34, Greek papyri dating pipeline (with B33). It is a staged recognition-to-date pipeline with a fragment-level aggregator, and B33 provides an error framework that accepts imprecision and compares models with humans. Test: copy the staging idea (per-page predictions aggregated to a document or folder-level estimate) and score with B33's interval-aware metric.
8. B17 and B16, HeidelTime and the BERT temporal tagger (B33 is folded into item 7). Test: run HeidelTime or the BERT tagger on OCR text as a cheap laptop-tier first stage, with a rule for explicit years, and measure how much of the 97.5% printed-year case it gets without any VLM. The remaining gap is the 63.4% case that needs inference.

## 5. Open questions for the deep read

1. Does B1 report per-document-length or per-genre accuracy, and does its fine-tuned versus frontier gap match our 41% against 63.4%?
2. Do any of these methods date from entity cues? I found no paper that links entities to Wikidata and intersects office-holding intervals. The deep read should look for entity-linking-plus-time work in a second source (my negative came from one search), then decide whether to build it, since Wikidata has start/end qualifiers (P580, P582) for offices.
3. Forward citations were not followed (Semantic Scholar was rate limited). Walk forward from B1, B6, B2, B3 and B7, and from the Müller-Budack photo paper (B40) for regression-versus-classification evidence.
4. Does anyone evaluate frontier VLMs on dating undated images of documents? I found nothing; the deep read should check ICDAR 2025/2026 workshop proceedings and DH 2025-2026 abstracts for it.
5. For calibration (B46): does split-conformal hold when calibration folders come from other collections? Needs a test across archives, not within one.
6. Is there a typeface or typewriter-model dating method with measured accuracy? None found. Forensic sources describe manual practice only (B44). A cheap test is whether a VLM can name typeface and an anachronistic font from a crop.
7. Which temporal-expression handler copes with archival forms ("the 5th inst.", "last Tuesday", dateline-only years)? B16, B17 and B18 were built for newswire; their behaviour on letters with relative dates and a missing reference year is unknown.
8. Does folder context help or hurt a one-shot VLM? B7 and B9 suggest ordering is feasible; no source measures the gain from neighbour dates in a prompt.
