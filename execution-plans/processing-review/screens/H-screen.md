## 3. READ shortlist (ranked)

1. **Kiessling, PP-OCRv6 for historical text (#1).** It is the strongest direct challenge to our design choice of VLM students. A compact CTC recogniser with heterogeneous pretraining beats a 9B Qwen3.5 recogniser on Latin script and is roughly 45 times faster (seen). It also reports few-shot adaptation by line count. Test: fine-tune a line recogniser on 40, 80 and 200 corrected lines from one of our boxes and compare CER and speed against our 2B student on the same lines. Decide whether the laptop profile should use a recogniser rather than a VLM for the handwriting pages.
2. **Churro (#4) with olmOCR (#16).** These are the two published teacher-student document recipes at our scale. Churro reaches 70.1% handwritten similarity at 3B and beats Gemini 2.5 Pro on handwriting. olmOCR measured student-teacher alignment. Test: report our students' agreement with the 27B teacher on held-out windows, and then their agreement with human truth. If the first is high and the second is lower, the students inherit teacher errors. Also run Churro, if weights are released, on our handwriting pages as a zero-effort baseline.
3. **Lightweight VLMs for OCR and structured JSON (#2).** It tests fine-tune against few-shot and single against pooled checkpoints, on heritage collections, with code. Test: repeat its pooled-versus-specific comparison, training one student on all page kinds and others on single kinds (our router already separates kinds), to see whether pooling helps or hurts as it did for Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
4. **In-context learning for HTR (#3).** A route to adaptation with no training: 16 in-context lines drop average IAM CER from 17.7% to 5.4%. Test: put 8 corrected lines from the same box in the prompt of the student and the teacher and measure the CER change on typed, handwritten and form pages. This is the cheapest "adapt to your collection" feature for the OpenRouter profile. Note the ICFHR2016 failure.
5. **LLMs for page stream segmentation (#5).** Our core segmentation task, with a benchmark. It reports tuned small LLMs above multimodal encoders and poor zero-shot GPT-4o (snippet). Test: run our student on TABME++ for a ready number, then give it a neighbour-page window of different sizes (1, 3, 5 pages) on our boxes and measure boundary F1, which also settles whether neighbouring pages help.
6. **UCCI (#6).** A tested way to calibrate the confidence signal and set the escalation threshold by cost. Test: fit isotonic regression on the student's token-margin score against teacher disagreement for 500 held-out windows, and measure how many windows escalate at a chosen accuracy. This replaces a hand-set threshold in the failure cascade and checks whether margin confidence works on our outputs at all (#15 warns it may not).
7. **mlx-vlm training plus Hugging Face skills (#7, #45).** Together they define the practical laptop and agent-driven kit. Test: on the 18 GB Mac, run `mlx_vlm.train` with LoRA on the 2B at 8-bit and QLoRA settings using 200 and 800 corrected windows, recording peak memory, time per epoch and CER change. It is unconfirmed that Qwen3.5 is trainable there; check that first. Then try packaging by `--adapter-path` against a merged model.
8. **Switchable adapters for manuscripts (#20).** A pattern for shipping one base model plus small per-task adapters, which suits user-trained add-ons to a shipped student. Test: train two adapters (typed memos, handwriting) on the 2B, and measure adapter size, swap time, and quality against one pooled adapter.

## 4. Benchmarks and datasets we could reuse

- **Churro-DS (#4):** 99,491 pages, 155 corpora, 22 centuries, 46 language clusters. Licence not seen; release was promised in the abstract. Close to our public 895-page set (older hands, print, non-Latin). Far from 20th-century typed US papers.
- **TABME / TABME++ (#5, #25):** page streams of business documents with OCR; licence not seen. Close to our segmentation task in structure (folders of mixed documents), far in period and capture (scans, not phone photographs). Tobacco800 (91.9% in #24) is the older, smaller companion.
- **MMLongBench-Doc (#27):** 135 documents, 1,091 questions, 47.5 pages on average. Modern PDFs. Useful only as a long-context sanity check for the teacher.
- **SynthDocBench (#28):** synthetic and controlled. Use to separate "needs neighbouring pages" from "model forgets in long context".
- **Chronicling America and NewsNet7 (#35, #55):** public-domain LoC pages; NewsNet7 has 3,000 layout-annotated pages from 21 states; ChroniclingAmericaQA adds corrected text. Close to our newspaper and clipping pages; page images are scans, not photographs.
- **Chronicling Germany (#35):** 801 annotated pages, 1617-1933; non-US but useful for layout.
- **Black newspapers 400-page set (#34):** scored without ground truth; method more than data.
- **CATMuS (named in search results, https://hal.science/ record seen):** large medieval Latin-script HTR set; far from our period, relevant to the handwriting student only.
- **IAM, Washington, RIMES, ICFHR2016, LAM, SaintGall (#3):** standard HTR sets, line crops, scanned; far from photographs of archival boxes but give numbers comparable with the literature.
- **Heritage collections in #2:** three university collections; availability is unknown until the repo is read.

None of these are phone photographs of boxes. All results transfer to our material only through our own test sets.

## 5. Open questions for the deep read

1. Does a heterogeneously pretrained small recogniser (#1) beat our 2B student on the handwriting pages after 40-200 corrected lines, and what does it do to latency and install size? If so, should the laptop route be a recogniser plus a VLM for layout and dates?
2. How much do students inherit teacher errors? Churro and olmOCR report alignment, but none of the screened work measures student error on cases where the teacher is wrong. What do they say about teacher label noise on handwriting?
3. How many human corrections change a VLM student measurably? We found no learning curve for VLMs. The nearest figures are vendor rules of thumb (Transkribus: 10,000 words per hand) and line counts for recognisers (40-80 lines, seen, unverified). In-context lines (#3) may beat gradient fine-tuning at the low end; where is the crossover?
4. Do neighbouring pages, folder metadata and finding aids improve segmentation, dating or OCR? We found PSS work that uses neighbours (#26, which did not win) and correction work that uses preceding text (#22, up to about 300 words), but nothing on finding-aid conditioning for archival boxes. This looks like an open gap we could fill with a small experiment.
5. Is token-margin confidence calibratable on our students (#6), given the warnings in #15 and the self-report failures in the cascade blog sources? What is the share of pages that escalate at a fixed accuracy?
6. Is Qwen3.5 2B or 4B trainable in mlx-vlm today, with what peak memory and time on an 18 GB machine? The mlx-vlm README gives inference peak memory only (18.3 GB for Qwen3.5-4B at 128k context). Does a LoRA trained on a GPU (our L40S) load as an adapter on the 8-bit MLX model without a quality loss?
7. Which packaging route survives for user-trained models: MLX adapter, merged MLX weights, GGUF, Core ML, or Core AI? Core AI needs macOS 27 and adapter swapping is undocumented (#48, #47). MLX Swift is the route with documented LoRA support.
8. Does pooled training hurt? #2 reports negative transfer for two of three models; our single student was trained on a pooled 5,885-window set. Does a per-page-kind adapter set recover quality?
9. What do institutions actually run? LoC chose Tesseract-based reprocessing (#32). We found no published Harvard, BPL, BL or NARA evaluation of VLM pipelines, so the claim "institutions have tested this" is unsupported by what we saw; check Impresso and Living with Machines directly.
