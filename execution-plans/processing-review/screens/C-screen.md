## 2. The two items the owner named

**HMBERT.** SEEN, not fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15575 (hmBERT: Historical Multilingual Language Models for NER, Schweter et al., 2022) and https://huggingface.co/dbmdz/bert-base-historic-multilingual-cased. It is a BERT-family pretrained language model for historical German, English, French, Swedish and Finnish, built for named-entity recognition at HIPE-2022. It is not a post-OCR corrector. It handled OCR noise only by dropping low-confidence text from its training corpora (confidence threshold 0.6-0.7). It is useful to us only as an encoder to fine-tune for noisy historical text, and it covers no handwriting and no non-European scripts. If the owner meant something that corrects OCR with a historical language model, the closest items found are HIPE-OCRepair-2026 and "No Free Lunches" below.

**University of Saskatchewan thesis.** FETCHED: https://harvest.usask.ca/items/a7a70e7d-1a6b-4cda-980a-7f5a6fb14bb4. Jacob W. Polay, "Loud Yet Invisible: A Humanist-Designed Pipeline for Unlocking the Early Modern Archive", MA History, University of Saskatchewan, issued 2026-08-17, advisor Jim Clifford. It is a three-stage pipeline built with open, locally run models: (1) transformer-based transcription replacing OCR, (2) a purpose-built NER model for people, places and commodities, (3) a retrieval system for aggregate historical questions. Corpus: about 20,000 early modern documents on the British Caribbean commodity trade. Reported: transcription error under 10 percent (about six times better than the pre-2016 OCR in online collections), NER F1 about 0.83 (above Gemini, per the page), retrieval 15.4 percent above the next-best method. The page does not name the transcription model and gives no code link. The PDF (4 MB, linked from the page; handle https://hdl.handle.net/10388/19063) was not read. It does not use hmBERT as far as the page shows. Its companion benchmark appears to be "Reading the Archive by Machine" (row 22), which uses Saskatchewan material (the "Sask" and "HHTR" sets) and is the most useful item for us.

## 4. READ shortlist (ranked)

1. **Reading the Archive by Machine (#3).** It is the only item that scores today's open parsers and Gemini on old print, newspapers and hands with a semantic error measure, and it reports loops and refusals. Test: re-run its released demonstration set (Zenodo) through our route set, and see whether GLM-OCR's weakness on early-modern type and olmOCR's spelling modernization show up in our 895-page set.
2. **CHURRO and CHURRO-DS (#1, #2).** An open 3B model trained on 99k pages of historical print and hands, with a stated 70.1 on handwriting against Gemini 2.5 Pro's 63.6. Test: run churro-3B on the 895 pages and on the 99 owner pages. If it matches LightOnOCR-3 4B on the hard set and loses on typed 1950-84 English, that is a routing signal. Check overlap between CHURRO-DS and our 895 set before trusting any score, and read the Qwen research licence.
3. **A Free Lunch? PP-OCRv6 for historical text (#11) with Kraken v5 (#12).** A compact fine-tuned line recogniser beat a large VLM on historical Latin hands. Test: segment lines on the hand-written subset, read with the Kraken PP-OCRv6 base models, and compare to the VLM at equal pages. This is the laptop-only profile's best chance.
4. **When Do VLMs Help Arabic Manuscript OCR? (#10).** It tests the staged claim directly: a classic OCR output as an anchor for a VLM corrector helps on readable input and hurts on bad input, and it argues for routing by script and OCR reliability. Test: the same cheap-reader-then-VLM variant on our non-Latin pages, scored per page against the one-shot VLM.
5. **HIPE-OCRepair 2026 with "No Free Lunches" (#5, #6).** Over-correction at low noise is the known failure of LLM post-correction. Test: add a post-correction stage only on pages where an OCR route scores below a threshold, and use alignment-based overgeneration removal. Measure NES change on the 99 owner pages, where the stage should do nothing.
6. **NewsBench (#36).** Direct evidence that detecting regions first lifts a frontier model from 0.820 to 0.936 and a specialist to 0.970 on dense newspaper pages. Test: run the 19 pages in whole-page and region-first modes with our best open and frontier readers; then repeat on the newspapers inside the 895 set.
7. **HunyuanOCR-1.5 anti-degeneration training (#30) with the loop engineering reports (#55-#57).** Our open models loop on 76-350 pages. These sources describe a training reward, a DPO method, and decoder penalties, and one case where the loop came from the port, not the model. Test: take the looping pages, rerun with a frequency penalty, a stricter token cap with repetition-stop and retry, and tiling; check first whether our runtime drops the position delta on large images.
8. **Polay thesis (#59).** Owner-named. It reports a local-model pipeline for early modern print at about 20,000 documents. Test: read the PDF for which transcription model and which gold sets, then see if its transcription model is among those already evaluated.

## 6. Open questions for the deep read

1. Does churro-3B hold up on our 895 pages, or does CHURRO-DS overlap with them? What is its score on the 99 typed owner pages?
2. Is the line-recogniser result (#11) a result about hands only, or does it hold on typed 1950-84 pages and on non-Latin scripts? What are its figures (the abstract page has none)?
3. For loops: how many of the 76-350 looping pages are the model, the decoder settings, the image size (compare the M-RoPE bug in #57), or a prompt mismatch? Does a frequency penalty, a repetition-stop-and-retry, or a tile split recover them?
4. Does region-first help or hurt each route on our material? NewsBench says it helps on dense newspapers; #3 says layout-aware tools win on columns but the OCR quality inside regions differs by tool. What happens on typed memos and forms where there is little layout?
5. Which confidence signal best predicts a page below, say, NES 0.7: token log-probability, a second open reader's disagreement, loop detection, or VL-Uncertainty-style perturbation? I found no paper that tests page routing for OCR; this must be measured by us.
6. Does LLM post-correction help any page of ours? The evidence says it over-corrects on clean input and fails on Finnish (#6), so test it only on routed low-score pages with alignment guards.
7. Which weights licences allow us to bundle or download the model in a distributed Mac app (Qwen research, Hunyuan community, Surya OpenRAIL-M)?
8. Are the vendor olmOCR-Bench figures (LightOnOCR-3 86.3, Chandra OCR 2 85.9, dots.mocr 83.9) comparable? Two vendors say the benchmark is saturated, and one flags a different method for LightOn. Do not use them to rank for our material.
9. What do the missing items hold: Docling and granite-docling, Transkribus current models, Party, the Medusa report, DeepSeek-OCR 2, Qwen3.5/3.6 OCR evidence, Infinity Parser 2 source?
