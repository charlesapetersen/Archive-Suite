## 1. Summary for the owner

The question the review set was whether to read a photographed page in one shot with a vision-language model or to stage the work, and the answer so far differs by stage and by who the user is.

**OCR.** On typed and printed 20th-century pages, which are most of the owner's corpus, one shot wins for every profile and the choice is price and failure rate: the top readers tie within two points on the 96 (Claude Sonnet 5.5 96.47, Gemini 3.1 Pro 94.85, Qwen3.6-27B 94.28 NES; C §5), and every open route reads the public typescripts at 89.7-96.0 (open sweep, L3). On dense multi-column pages a layout stage first is decisive for readers under about 10B and for the OCR-specialist models, and nil for 27B-class and frontier readers: on the 15 NewsBench pages GLM-OCR goes from 29.2 NES whole-page to 0.969-0.973 on DocLayout-YOLO regions, Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite from 0.820 to 0.961, while Qwen3.6-27B scores 0.967 whole and 0.965 on regions (E claim 34). The staged route is also seventeen times cheaper on a cluster (1,087 pages per GPU-hour against 65; E claim 35). So for the laptop and cheap-API profiles the layout stage is how a 1B reader does a 27B reader's work on newspapers; for the premium profile it is unnecessary. On hands, one shot with Gemini 3.8 Flash for the API profiles (95.97 on the 16 handwritten truth pages), LightOnOCR-3 4B at 89.67 for the laptop, and line-level script specialists for non-Latin and early modern hands where they beat the open page models by 11-37 points (C §5-6). The largest defect on every open route is repetition loops, not misreading: 76-350 of 895 pages per route are capped, almost all loops, and the answered-page score sits 10-13 points above the overall score (open sweep). Nobody has measured a layout stage on an ordinary letter, memo or form; the one proxy says a born-digital detector loses a handwritten page (OmniDocBench notes mAP 9.54; E claim 9).

**Enhancement and dewarping.** The field's evidence is that restoration helps old engines and barely helps frontier readers: appearance restoration halved Tesseract's error and moved GPT-4o from 6.51 to 6.06 CER (PreP-OCR, D §5); dewarping took a 7B VLM from MMCER 0.576 to 0.215 on extremely curled pages (DvD, D §5). Nothing has been measured on phone photographs of archival paper or on a 2025-26 model. Our own pages' real defects are not optical: the six worst typed originals are hurt by neighbours and labels in the frame, show-through and faint type, none by blur, shadow or glare, and sharpness does not correlate with the score on any route (L claims 42-43). Under the owner's rule the reading copy may take only geometry-only methods, which keep a backward grid that is one line of code from being saved (UVDoc, DocRes; D §5), and the content-removing restorers are OCR-input candidates for the laptop and pipeline readers only. Expected gains: real for Apple Vision and PP-OCRv6, small or nil for Gemini and the 27B.

**Text placement.** Aligning a position-free reader's text onto Apple Vision's lines works on print (Falcon-OCR through align: 1.20% to 0.63% words wrong on 56 pages) and fails on handwriting (65% of words placed; 96 missed per 1,000 as read becomes 367 in the PDF), because Vision finds about 78% of handwritten lines (F §5). The staged alternative, a baseline segmenter trained on archival hands plus forced alignment, has never been run on our pages and is the lever to pull before the text page is accepted as the permanent fallback for hands.

**Marks.** Unmeasured everywhere. The practical unit is a box, not a pixel mask (pixel separation reaches IoU 0.50-0.71 on 20th-century typescript with handwriting; K claims 8, 11); the candidates are a YOLO11 detector trained on AnnoPage's two classes plus SignverOD, and a frontier model asked for boxes. Both need a labelled set that does not exist.

**Segmentation.** Deciding each boundary from a pair or window of pages beats single-page classification, in the field and in our history (A §2). The cloud windows put 87-92% of pages in an exactly right document on the locked boxes, and the review design matters more than the model: Claude's confidence brings 97.3% after 10.2 checks per 100 pages, Gemini's 100% after 24.2, the trained 2B on the Mac 78.8% unchecked and 78.8 checks per 100 pages to reach 97.3% (`options.md`). The 35-47% on the Lincoln Papers is the Qwen family only; every true boundary was found and the loss is 32-44 false splits inside long manuscripts (A §5), and no cloud model has run on Lincoln. That is the single most important unknown in the review: if Gemini and Claude hold near 90% there, the laptop route's weakness is on handwriting and the fix is routing; if they fall too, the material needs a continuity cue nothing carries. "Staged" here means a cheap local layer (threshold, boundary prior, doc-split-v1's calibration, the salutation and closing cues at 90% precision and 24% recall; G §3) under a cloud voter, with two-model disagreement, not one model's confidence, choosing what the user reviews.

**Dating.** Reading the year from text is near-solved by every route when the date is in the text (94-97.5%; B §Comparison). The 63.4% on unprinted years is a mixture: 81.8% where the previous document carries the same year in its own text (the strict enclosure case, 33 of 131) and 51.8% where no printed same-year document sits within three positions (56 of 131), where the small models collapse to 16-25% (B claims table). Enclosure detection has no literature, and the app's own `[enclosure]` marker has never been measured. The staged pipeline to test is rule candidates, a small model choosing among them, a relation classifier that quotes its cue, propagation by the existing `EnclosureDates`, and only the residue to a paid route with a traceable-evidence prompt and a conformal interval; `Date Uncertain` must be scored as two error rates, because a constant "certain" scores the 80.0% the trained models get.

**Entities, kinds, capture, training.** The tagging prompt already extracts author and recipient; the fields have no truth and no consumer, Gemini's names are in the document's own text about four times in five (the 81/9/10 split is [?]; strict match 74.0%), and names alone do not separate enclosures (50% against 43%; G). Page-kind routing is worth under a point at the premium tier (97.26 routed against 96.47 best single reader) and matters on the cheap routes; "mixed" is 2% or 8% of the corpus by definition (K §5). Phone capture costs a VLM about nine points on modern print and a pipeline fourteen (PureDocBench; L claim 2), our own phone-versus-processed gap is 0.1-8.2 points by route (L claim 41), and the companions set nothing and check nothing (L claim 37). A training kit is sensible for a segmentation refit from tens of labels, a kraken fine-tune from 40-240 corrected lines, and a segmentation LoRA on a cluster served as a private voter; a VLM OCR fine-tune from small data has failed everywhere it has been tried, and Apple's adapter toolkit ended with macOS 26 (H §2, §6.3).

**What is already measured.** Segmentation on 914 owner pages and two public boxes with five cloud and local methods; dating on 300 owner documents with Gemini and seven open-model rows; OCR on the 96 with 29 routes and on the 895 with eleven open routes plus two hosted rows on the 440; the resolution ladder; the kind classifier; the placement of model text on Vision lines for print and hands; the entity fill rates; the salutation cue over 35,563 gaps. The eight 96-page open-model runs that complete the both-sets rule are on FarmShare awaiting collection (`truth96-farmshare-20261020.md`).

**What to test first, and why.** First the free things that change how existing results are read: the field's segmentation scorers over every cached answer (so Lincoln's severity under our metric and the correction-effort numbers the field publishes both exist), the OCR scorer additions (added words, cMER, order), and the 96's truth-bias audit (whether the 96 can rank readers at all; its truth stands 97% on the two drafters' agreement). Second the cheapest owner hour: classing the 131 unprinted-year documents, which unlocks every dating test. Third the two experiments that settle the biggest defaults: loop recovery on the capped pages (free, FarmShare now, the largest open-route gain on the table) and the cloud windows on Lincoln and CREST (about $1.40 or $0 through the plan routes). Fourth, regions against whole page by kind, which is the OCR form of the staged question. Then the labelled sets every later test needs (marks, capture tags, placement, entities), drafted by models and checked by the owner only where they disagree, and the dewarp and enhancement runs on the 96 that decide the reading-copy option. Everything that needs Sherlock (OpenPSS, Infinity Parser 2, the full degradation ladder) waits without blocking anything.

## 3. Benchmarks: what answers enough, what to borrow and run, what to build

Per stage. "Answers enough" means we need not build our own for that question. "Borrow and run" means the data, metric or scorer is reused on our setup because ours differs (photographs not scans, our resolution and JPEG, our prompts and routes) or because the published scores predate the current models. "Build" means nothing public applies.

**Image preparation.** Answers enough: the UVDoc 50-page benchmark, DIR300 and DocUNet with AD and LD rank dewarpers against each other (D §5); we do not rebuild that ranking, we reproduce the published 0.785 / 0.119 once to prove our installs. Borrow and run: DvD's MMCER protocol (reader-transcript CER between processed and reference) fits our truth scorer directly; DocPTBench's three-arm design (original, photographed, unwarped), its data licence unstated so measure with it and do not redistribute (E §5); Real5's warp and skew folders (images Apache-2.0, annotations under OmniDocBench's research-only terms, acceptable for internal measurement). Build: whether to dewarp at all on our pages, which needs the 96 and the marks set, since every public set is a whole flat-lay modern print under strong curl (D §2). Licence notes: UVDoc benchmark data licence unstated; DIR300 terms unstated; AnyPhotoDoc6300 gated under GPL-3.0 (D §4).

**Enhancement as OCR input.** Answers enough: nothing; every published gain is measured with Tesseract or a CTC engine, and the one VLM figure (PreP-OCR, GPT-4o 6.51 to 6.06) is on scanned early books (D §5). Borrow and run: RealDAE as the closest appearance set (600 camera-captured pairs; licence unstated); OSR, Kligler and Jung for shadow (the only shadow-removal OCR figures, Tesseract only; licences unstated, cite rather than build on; L §4); DIBCO for binarisation truth on scans; HIPE-OCRepair's cMER scorer and its level-0 data as the abstention test for any corrector (dta19 CC BY-SA 4.0; icdar2017 and impresso CC BY-NC-SA; overproof research only; C §4). Build: the added-words, rewritten-words and restoration-only-words rates on the 96 (D §6.4), which no public metric provides; the two-seed hallucination calibration (D test 7); CHAOS-Bench's altered-word probe copied as a method on 20 typed owner pages (C §5). Contamination: none applies to our own pages.

**Layout and reading order.** Answers enough: Real5-OmniDocBench for photographed robustness of layout modules on modern print (five conditions, twenty published rows); RT-DocLayout's Table 2 is the one published claim that would change a decision (DocLayout-YOLO 48.74 on skew against 91.66), and it is checked, not rebuilt (E §5); cBAD 2019 for ranking line detectors on European archival hands (Orli's paper already places Orli, Planet, DocExtractor and ParseNet; F §5). Borrow and run: the 40-page historical reading-order benchmark (MIT; `harness/score.py`, tau with a validity guard, order-induced word error) as the public anchor for any ordering stage, adding our pages to it rather than building a second harness (E §5); NewsBench's configurations and `overall`, `bowF1`, `chrF`, `gap` scorer, since NewsBench is already our L9 (MIT code, CC0 data); OmniDocBench's reading-order edit distance; American Stories' class set and headline-to-article linking rule for clippings (CC-BY); OmniDocBench's handwritten-notes subset as the proxy for handwriting harm (research-only annotations). Build: a layout stage on ordinary archive pages by kind, which no source has measured (E §8), and order on annotated pages, which needs the marks set. Contamination: NewsBench's pages are public LoC scans with public volunteer transcriptions; CHURRO-DS includes American Stories but not By the People; no model card read lists NewsBench (C §5). Not worth borrowing: DocEng photographed sets (binarisation only), RealDoc-Bench-Layout (scanned regulated forms; the commercial comparison only), Wild-OmniDocBench (unlicensed zip), M6Doc (non-commercial, by request) (E §5).

**OCR and HTR.** Answers enough: clean 19th-century print and early modern English print are settled by Reading the Archive's BLN600 and Jacob tables (top readers within a point; C claims 14-15); the medieval Latin-script specialist case by Kiessling's Table 4; nothing for phone capture, for which no source read measures photographed paper against scans (C §8). Do not borrow: CHURRO-DS scores (truths cleaned with Gemini 2.5 Pro, the model it is compared against; research-only licence), but do hash its 1,170 test pages against our 895, since eight of our fifteen strata draw from its source corpora and the specialists note's overlap column should add READ 2017 (L6) and Bentham (L2) (C claim 11, §5); socOCRbench (truth method undisclosed); vendor OmniDocBench figures as a guide to our task (no photographs, and v1.5 and v1.6 are different test sets; I §9).
