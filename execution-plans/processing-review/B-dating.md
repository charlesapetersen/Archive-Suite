# Strand B: dating

Deep read, 2026-10-20, re-centred twice the same day on the owner's corrections. First: a document tagged with a year that does not itself contain a date is, in the owner's corpus, mostly an enclosure that carries the date of the covering document it was sent with; the owner wants explicit dating (dates written or printed on the document, and dates inherited from an explicitly dated covering document when the relation can be established). Second: estimating a year from neighbours is acceptable when marked `Date Uncertain`, and dating from typeface or paper would be welcome if it were feasible, on one condition: every non-explicit suggestion must be traceable, with the evidence it rests on (which neighbouring document, which cue, quoted or located) and a confidence, because untraceable guesses invite hallucination. The note therefore has two centres, explicit extraction and enclosure detection, and a third section on non-explicit estimation judged by traceability and calibration.

## Question

The Processor tags each document with a year and, where the document states it, a month and day; it never estimates the month; where the year is estimated it adds `Date Uncertain`. Since W37 an enclosure can also carry its covering letter's date as a `Sent With` token, proposed only when the OCR model writes `[enclosure]` on the enclosure's first page and the segmenter confirms the unit before it is a document. On 300 owner-tagged documents Gemini 3.8 Flash gets the exact year on 97.5% where the owner's year string is in the ABBYY text sent and on 63.4% where it is not; the Qwen3.5 2B and 4B fine-tunes get 95% and 41%, and never mark a date uncertain. The strand asks: how far explicit date extraction can be pushed, from text and from the image (partial dates, letterheads, postmarks, received stamps, handwritten annotations); whether the field has anything for detecting that one document was sent with another and for propagating the covering document's date; how the 131 unprinted-year documents divide into enclosures of a dated document and the rest; and, for whatever is not explicit, how a method can show its evidence and a calibrated confidence so that the owner can check it.

## What the field does now

### Explicit date extraction from text and image

Dates in running text are handled by temporal taggers. HeidelTime (B17) is a rule-based tagger in 13 hand-built languages that normalises expressions to TIMEX3 values, including partial ones (year-month, day-month without a year), and needs a document creation time to resolve relative expressions such as "the 5th inst." (repository page). The BERT tagger (B16) marks DATE/TIME/DURATION/SET spans at 0.1B parameters but does not normalise them and its card says its raw output "might be noisy and hard to decipher" (model card). Neither decides which of several dates on a page is the document's own date; that choice is the whole difficulty on a letter that mentions earlier letters, a letterhead's founding year, or a received stamp. The key-information-extraction benchmarks score a date field on receipts and invoices (SROIE, DocILE) and on long typed documents (Kleister), never on letters; the two sources that do extract a letter's send date are the humanities benchmark (G17: German business letters 1926-1935, fields send date, sender, receiver, scoring unspecified on the README) and the Hammer-Purgstall study (G16: 100 letters stratified by presence of a dateline; Claude Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.2 far above a local Qwen3 14B; all models weak when the dateline is absent; no per-field date figure published).

Handwritten dates are a solved recognition problem when the date region is given. DARE trains an EfficientNetV2 multi-head classifier on 2.2 million segmented handwritten dates from Danish and Swedish records and reaches sequence accuracies of 92% to over 99% on its own sources, against 74.9% for Transkribus on the same death certificates (Section 4, Table; Section 4 text); pre-training on DARE lifts a new source (Swedish grade sheets) from 85.17% trained from scratch to 95.63% (Section 5 table). What DARE does not do is find the date on a page: its inputs are crops from tabular records. Date-region detection on free-form pages exists only as a thesis (DateFinder, positional expectancy on diaries) and older cheque work. Stamps can be detected (YOLO variants on the StaVer and DDI-100 sets reach about 98% mAP on 732 images, per a search summary, [?]) but no dataset reads the date inside a received stamp, and no postmark-reading dataset was found. The scholarly editing practice, in the TEI "Encoding Correspondence" handbook, treats the postmark as the dispatch date and records it in `correspDesc` beside the sender's own date when they differ, and records inferred dates with `@evidence` (internal, external, conjecture), `@cert` and `notBefore`/`notAfter` (chapters "Postage Stamps, Seals, and Postmarks" and "Uncertainties in Metadata"); its chapter on attachments is listed as in preparation.

Vision-language models read all of these in one pass, which is what our Gemini route does, and the literature on their evidence is new: CiteVQA (2026) requires element-level bounding-box citations with each answer over 1,897 questions on 711 PDFs and finds "attribution hallucination", right answers with wrong regions cited, with the strongest system (Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview) at a strict attributed accuracy of 76.0 and the best open model at 22.5 (abstract).

### Detecting that a document was sent with another

Nothing published does this. Three searches (page-stream and scanned-document literature; transmittal and cover letters with attachment linking; archival description of enclosures) and the entities screen's searches returned no method, dataset or metric for cover-letter-to-enclosure relations. The nearest fields are page-stream segmentation (strand A), whose datasets are synthetic concatenations of whole documents with no relation between them (DocSplit's ground-truth schema has `group_id` and `parent_doc_name` for the packet, no cover or attachment field, Appendix B.4), and email, whose attachment structure is given by the format rather than inferred. Two resources exist without a method. The IIT-CDIP tobacco collection carries an `<ag>` "Attachment Group" element in 1,274,713 of 6.9 million records, which the data-elements note does not explain ("I'm not sure what this is", Section IV.B), beside a document-date element `<YR>`/`<dd>` with "a substantial number of errors and dummy values"; if the attachment groups are what the name says, they are the only public set of typed 20th-century documents with attachment relations, and its licence is unverified. Archival description (DACS, the Archives of American Art guidelines) asks the archivist to estimate dates for undated material ("probably 1867", "circa 1975") and says nothing specific about enclosures (search summary, [?]). The TEI handbook's attachments chapter is unwritten.

What the app has is therefore ahead of the literature and unmeasured: the OCR prompt's optional `[enclosure]` marker ("a separate document with its OWN date or heading ... clearly sent WITH the covering letter on the previous pages, e.g. that letter says 'I enclose...'", `OCRPrompt.swift`), parsed header-only, turned into `DocumentSegment.enclosureOf` only when the preceding unit is a document and never across a box or folder label, chained so consecutive enclosures point at one cover, and dropped when a review changes the boundary; the cover's own date then becomes `Sent With` (`DocumentSegmenter.swift`). Its known limits: proposals come only from that marker, so Mistral, Apple Vision and pre-OCRed runs propose none; nothing checks that the cover is a letter; the manual sheet can clear a relation but not re-point it (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md`). No precision or recall of the marker has been measured; the only test is the parser contract (`ocr-prompt-app.md`).

### Non-explicit estimation, and whether it can be traced

Text dating as classification: B1 shows proprietary models with five labelled examples at macro-F1 0.74-0.96 on 100-token book and magazine snippets against 0.61-0.78 for LoRA-tuned 4-9B models (Tables 3, 4); Decoding the Past (B8) reaches 76.7% century and 26.1% decade accuracy on 18,984 books (abstract); the charters method (G36) reaches a 6.0-year median error from shingle prevalence learned on 2,608 dated documents (Table 2). All three date from language change over decades or centuries and are untraceable in the owner's sense: the evidence is a feature vector, not a quotable cue. Dating by neighbours has one precedent for web pages (Nunes et al. 2007, cited by B1, not read) and none for archival boxes.

Calibration: FermiEval (B46) finds nominal 99% intervals from GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.5 Haiku and Grok cover the answer 65% of the time, and that split-conformal widening restores coverage and cuts the Winkler interval score by 25-59% depending on the level (abstract; "Scoring all of the Models"); the Bayesian elicitation study (B47) finds nominal 95% intervals from eleven models cover 9-44%, that model size and not reasoning effort drives accuracy (Opus 4.5 median error 23% against Haiku 4.5 at 90%), and that conformal recalibration with at least 15 calibration points per group recovers coverage by widening intervals two to five times (Sections IV-B, IV-C, V-B). Selective prediction, keeping only the most certain answers, is the other lever: B2 reports that keeping the 20% most certain patches cuts its MAE from 5.4 to 0.5 years (Table 3), within the codex caveat above.

Traceability: the literature on model-stated rationales is a warning. Turpin et al. (2023) show chain-of-thought explanations that justify a biased answer without mentioning the bias, with accuracy drops of up to 36% across 13 BIG-Bench Hard tasks for GPT-3.5 and Claude 1.0 (abstract). Lanham et al. (2023) find faithfulness falls as models grow more capable and varies by task (abstract). Chen et al. (2025) find reasoning models verbalise a hint they used in at least 1% but often under 20% of cases (abstract). For cited evidence, Liu et al. (2023) find that four generative search engines fully support only 51.5% of their sentences and that 74.5% of citations support their sentence (abstract); ALCE finds the best models "lack complete citation support 50% of the time" on ELI5 (abstract); CiteVQA finds attribution hallucination in document QA (above). The consequence for us is that a rationale is not evidence: the evidence must be a quoted string or a located region that a scorer can check against the page or the neighbour, and the test must score that check, not the prose.

Visual cues: the measured work is on medieval and ancient hands (B2, B3, B33, B37) and photographs; nothing measures typeface, typewriter, letterhead or paper as a dating cue on 20th-century material, and forensic practice does it by hand against type-font databases (B44). The one framing that is traceable by construction is retrieval (B37): a document is dated by its nearest dated neighbour in an embedding, and the neighbour is the evidence. B2's spatial uncertainty maps and GradCAM are post-hoc and, by its own Limitations, do not flag an unseen hand.

## What our 300 documents say, and what transfers

The owner's statement is partly borne out and partly refined by the run. Of the 131 year-tagged documents whose owner year is not in the text sent, 33 (25%) sit immediately after a document that carries the same year in its own text, which is the strict enclosure-consistent case; 29 more follow a same-year document that is itself unprinted, which is what chains of enclosures behind one cover look like; 75 (57%) have a printed same-year document within three positions on either side; 35 have one further away in the folder; and 21 (16%) have none in the folder at all. Cue words in the previous document ("enclose", "attached", "herewith") appear in only 11 of 131 cases and in 5 of the 33 strict ones, so a relation detector that waits for the cover to announce the enclosure will miss most relations, and the OCR prompt's marker, which asks for exactly that announcement, is likely to have low recall. The collection folders span many years (107 of 131 have more than five distinct owner years), so a folder-level prior cannot replace the relation: the carrier of the date is the adjacent document, not the folder.

The models already behave as if inheriting. On the 33 strict cases Gemini gives the previous document's year 82% of the time and is exactly right 82% of the time; the 27B is right 67%, the 4B 61%. On the 56 documents with no printed same-year document within three positions, Gemini falls to 52% exact and the open models to 16-25%, while Gemini stays within one year on 91% of them. So the 63.4% headline is a mixture: about half of the unprinted-year documents are dated by an adjacent or near explicit date that the models mostly pick up, and the other half are dated by something the text route cannot see, where small models collapse. The 21 documents with no printed same-year document anywhere in the folder are the cases to look at by hand: the owner's year there came from the page image (a handwritten or stamped date ABBYY did not read), from a cover more than ten positions away, or from knowledge outside the folder.

What transfers from the literature. Explicit extraction from text is near-solved for the year by every route we measured (94-97.5%), consistent with the field's view that dates in text are a tagging problem; the residual is the choice among several dates and the month, where the owner's month was in the text for none of Gemini's 27 misses. For dates on the image, DARE shows handwritten date crops are read at 92-99% once located, and that transfer from its model lifts a new source by about ten points; locating dates on free-form pages and reading received stamps and postmarks have no measured method, and the TEI practice says to keep a postmark or received date as a separate role, which our tag vocabulary does not have (only `Sent With` is a second role). For enclosure detection the field has nothing, so our own marker, measured for the first time by Test 1, is the state of the art by default. For non-explicit estimation the calibration recipe transfers directly (B46, B47: ask for an interval, widen it by a conformal quantile from a few dozen owner-dated documents per stratum), and the faithfulness literature says the evidence a model cites must be verified by a scorer, which Test 4 does. The published visual-dating accuracies (B2, B3, B33, B37) do not transfer: they measure script drift over centuries; within a box the only visual signal with a precedent is retrieval of a look-alike dated neighbour, which is traceable because the neighbour is the evidence.

Scorer changes this implies. `Date Uncertain` agreement should be reported as two error rates (owner-certain flagged uncertain; owner-uncertain flagged certain), because a constant "certain" scores 80.0%, which is the trained models' figure. Unprinted-year accuracy should be reported by the four structural classes below, because a single 63% hides a split from 82% to 52%. For owner-inferred years (year+DU, decade) the truth is an estimate, so a within-one-year or interval score (the HDC distance-from-interval, B33's tolerance curve) belongs beside the exact score. No public benchmark replaces our set; the one borrowable asset for a second, independent set is IIT-CDIP, after its licence and date noise are checked.

## Strand questions

### 1. Explicit extraction and normalisation from text and image

Text. Stage one of any route should be deterministic and local: candidate dates from the document's own text (four-digit years 1800-2030, month names and numerals, day-month-year and month-day-year forms, two-digit years with an apostrophe, partial forms year-month and month-day), with each candidate's position and surrounding line kept as its evidence. Stage two chooses the document's own date among the candidates, which is where the errors live: a dateline date against a letterhead's "established 1902", a received stamp (later than the document), a reference to an earlier letter, an enclosure list. A small model choosing among candidates with their context is an easier task than producing a year, and it keeps the evidence (the chosen string and its line) by construction; a rule can handle the common cases (a date in the first ten lines or beside a signature outranks one in the body; a date preceded by "received" or inside a stamp-like line is a receipt date, not the document date). Normalisation should follow TIMEX3 or EDTF for partial and uncertain values [I], mapped onto the tag facets: year only, year plus month, plus day, decade; a month is written only when it is on the document, as the spec says. A received date or postmark has no facet; if it is to be kept it belongs in the segment JSON sidecar, never as a second bare year that could cross-pair with the month (the W37 design record says why a second date must be one whole token).

Image. The owner's month was in the text for none of Gemini's 27 missed months, and 21 of 131 unprinted years have no printed same-year document in the folder, so a share of explicit dates is on the page and not in ABBYY's text: handwritten dates and annotations, received stamps, postmarks, dates in letterhead blocks, dates on a later page. A vision model given the page can read these in one pass, and the test for it is not only accuracy but location: the reply must quote the date string and say where it is (a line of its own transcription, or a box), and the scorer checks the quote against the model's transcription or the box against the page. The specialist route is detection then recognition: a detector for date regions and stamps (stamp detectors exist and are cheap; date-region detection on free-form pages does not exist and would need a few hundred of our own boxes), then DARE-style recognition fine-tuned from its public model on our crops. That route is for the laptop profile and is worth building only if Test 2 finds that image-only dates are a large share.

### 2. Detecting enclosure, attachment and sent-with relations, and propagating the date

The rules already settled (02-truth-check.md, approved 2026-10-05; SPEC W37) fix what a relation has to do: an enclosure with its own date or heading is its own document and, when the relation is established, carries `Sent With <cover's own date>`; an undated, unheaded attachment stays part of the letter and needs no relation; the cover never takes the enclosure's date; an undated cover yields no sent-with date; a relation never crosses a box or folder label. The owner's corpus tags predate this and encode inheritance directly as the enclosure's Year tag, which is why the strict class above is identifiable from tags and order alone.

Signals a detector can use, in order of how traceable they are: (a) the cover announces the enclosure ("I enclose", "attached", "herewith", a list of enclosures, "Enc." at the foot), present in only about one strict case in six in our sample; (b) the enclosure's own form, a report, memo, clipping or list with a heading and no dateline, following a letter; (c) continuity of names (the cover's subject or a named document matches the enclosure's title; the enclosure's author is named in the cover), which the automatic prompt's author and recipient fields already extract; (d) order and adjacency within the folder, which is the owner's own cue and the weakest alone. The app uses (a) as perceived by the OCR model page by page; a relation classifier that sees the cover's text and the candidate's first page together, and returns yes/no with the quoted cue and a confidence, would use all four and be checkable. Propagation is then mechanical and already built (`EnclosureDates`): `Sent With` takes the cover's own date at its precision, the enclosure's Year tag is the cover's year with `Date Uncertain`, and the confidence of the relation, not of the date, is what should be displayed and thresholded. An uncertain relation is the case for `Sent With Date Uncertain`, which the spec already has.

### 3. A test design on our 300 documents

Units are the tag-rule segments in folder order, as the bench builds them (box and folder photographs are segments; a relation never crosses one). Truth comes from the owner's tags and the order. For each of the 131 unprinted-year documents assign one class: A, strict: the previous segment is a document whose owner year is in its own text and equals this document's owner year (33); A', chained: the previous document has the same owner year but no printed year, and an A case lies within three positions before (a subset of the 29); B, near: a printed same-year document within three positions on either side, not A or A' (the remainder of 75); C, far: the nearest printed same-year document is four or more positions away in the folder (35); D, none: no printed same-year document in the folder (21). Then an owner pass on a sample, timed by the owner-review rule: confirm for A and A' whether the previous document is in fact the cover (and whether the month tags match, which happened in only 2 of 33, so the owner mostly inherited the year alone), and for D say where the year came from (image, box label, knowledge). That pass turns A into a relation truth set and D into an image-extraction truth set.

Scores. For relation detection: precision and recall of the proposed relation against A and A' (the OCR marker on the enclosure's first page; a relation classifier over cover-plus-candidate text), with the false positives inspected, since a relation wrongly proposed propagates a wrong date with a confident label. For propagation: the share of A and A' documents whose `Sent With` year equals the owner's year, and whose Year tag plus `Date Uncertain` match the owner's tags. For the year itself: exact and within-one accuracy per class A to D and per model, as the run above already gives. For `Date Uncertain`: by class, the two error rates; under the owner's rule every B, C and D document should carry it, and an A document should carry it unless the relation is confirmed. The 119 in-text documents stay as the extraction set, scored on year, month and the chosen-candidate evidence.

### 4. Non-explicit estimation, judged by traceability and calibration

What a traceable estimate looks like. The reply names the year, the evidence (the identifier of the neighbouring document and a quoted string from it, or a quoted cue from this document, or a located region), and a confidence or an interval; `Date Uncertain` is set by construction. The scorer checks three things independently: accuracy (exact, within one, within the stated interval); evidence validity (the cited neighbour exists in the context given, the quote is a substring of its text, and that neighbour carries the year claimed, or the cited region contains a date string); and calibration (coverage of the stated interval after split-conformal widening on a held-out half, per stratum, at least 15 documents per stratum per B47, and the Winkler score). A reply whose year is right but whose evidence fails the check counts as unsupported, which is the attribution-hallucination case CiteVQA measures and the owner's objection. Our run gives the prior: Gemini's year equals the previous document's year on 82% of strict cases, so when it cites that neighbour the citation will usually be true; the test is whether it cites the right one when the carrier is two or three documents away, and what it cites on the 21 documents that have no printed carrier at all, where any confident citation is suspect.

How methods expose evidence. Retrieval exposes it by construction (the retrieved dated neighbour, textual or visual, is the evidence; B37's framing). A candidate-choice extractor exposes the chosen string and its line. A relation classifier exposes the quoted cue. A vision model exposes a quote or a box, which CiteVQA shows is right for the best model about three quarters of the time and for open models far less. Free-text rationales expose nothing checkable, and the faithfulness papers say they should not be trusted as evidence.

Calibration. Ask for `low` and `high` at 90% with the year; compute the nonconformity max(low minus truth, truth minus high) on a calibration half; widen by its ceil((1-alpha)(n+1))-th smallest value; report coverage and width on the other half and swap. Stratify by class A to D and by model, because the error distributions differ by tens of points. Our aggregates say where Gemini's quantile will land on unprinted years: 91.6% within one year and all but about two of 131 within five, so a 90% interval will be about plus or minus one year and a 95% interval plus or minus two to five; the small models' intervals will be wider and still honest. Selective prediction follows from the same numbers: a threshold on the interval width or the stated confidence decides which non-explicit years are written with `Date Uncertain` and which are left for the owner, and the test reports accuracy at each retained share, as B2 does.

Interval and selective losses for a trained model (B3's interval Huber, B2's evidential head) would let a small model learn to widen rather than guess; they do not add knowledge, and the run above says the small models' failure on classes C and D is a knowledge failure. They are a second-order item after Tests 1-4.

### 5. Visual cues: typeface, typewriter, letterhead, paper

No published method gives a measured accuracy on 20th-century material, with or without traceable evidence. The measured visual dating is on medieval and ancient hands (B2, B3, B33) and on newspapers (B37, with the training-on-test caveat), and the one honest reading of those results is that within a collection the model recognises the writer or the codex rather than the period (B33 Section 5.2; B2 Limitations). Typeface release dates give lower bounds decades wide and are applied by hand in forensic practice (B44); whether a vision model can name a typewriter typeface from a crop is untested. The one traceable form of visual dating is retrieval of a look-alike dated page in the same box (same letterhead, same typewriter, same hand), where the evidence is the retrieved page; it is cheap to test on a Mac and is Test 6. The owner's doubt is shared.

## Open questions

1. For the 33 strict cases, is the previous document the cover, and for the 21 none cases, where did the year come from? Only the owner can say; both answers set the truth for Tests 1 and 2.
2. What is the recall of a cue-based relation detector when only one cover in six announces its enclosure? Test 1 answers it; if recall is low, the relation classifier needs the enclosure's form and the shared names, not the cover's words.
3. Which second-role dates should be kept at all (received stamp, postmark, filing date), and where? The tag vocabulary has one second role (`Sent With`); anything else would live in the sidecar. A product question for Phase 4.
4. Does a model that is asked for evidence become less accurate or less willing to answer? B47 saw refusals rise with some settings and web search degrade accurate models; Test 4 should report the invalid-reply rate beside accuracy.
5. Does conformal calibration from our collections transfer to another user's boxes of the same era? The guarantee needs exchangeability; a cross-collection check (calibrate on half the collections, test on the rest) decides whether the training kit (strand H) must ship a calibration step.
6. What are the Industry Documents Library's terms of use, and what does its attachment-group field mean? Needed before IIT-CDIP can be a second relation or dating set.
7. How should the Reader show a traceable estimate: the cited neighbour, the quote and the interval beside the italic year? The tag contract should not change; the sidecar and the PDF header can carry it.
